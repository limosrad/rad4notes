## Properties of radiation

### Fundamental properties of electromagnetic radiation

- Wave-particle duality: properties similar to both classical waves and particles.
- Classical wave properties:
    - Sinusoidally varying electric and magnetic fields with vectors perpendicular to each other, governed by Maxwell equations
    - Speed of EM radiation ($c$) is constant in all reference frames (Einstein’s special theory of relativity), equal to $299792458 \text{ ms}^{-1}$ (~ $3 \times 10^8 \text{ ms}^{-1}$).
    - The frequency ($f$) and wavelength ($λ$) of EM radiation is related as
    $$
    c = f λ
    $$
    - Like other waves, EM radiation shows reflection, refraction, interference, diffraction and polarisation.
- Classical particle properties:
    - EM radiation is transmitted and absorbed as discrete energy packets – which gives it particle-like behaviour – called photons.
    - Photons are massless but carry energy based on wavelength as
    $$
    E = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = \frac{1.24}{\lambda}
    $$
    - Here $E$ is in keV and $λ$ in nm

- Visible light has a wavelength of 400-700 nm while diagnostic x-rays have wavelengths of 0.0087-0.031 nm (40-140 keV).

### Properties related to x-ray interaction with matter:

- X-rays travel in a straight line (like other EM radiation).
- The intensity of a beam of x-rays emitted from a point source follows the inverse square law
    $$
    I \propto \frac{1}{d^2}
    $$
- X-rays are usually created in a spectrum, i.e., having a range of energies.
- Low energy x-rays have &uarr; absorption (&uarr; interaction with matter) &rarr; &uarr; mean energy of the x-ray beam as it passes through matter (‘beam hardening’).


## X-ray tube construction

### Principles of x-ray production

| Electron interaction with heavy metal atom | Produces |
| --- | --- |
| With outer shell electrons | Heat |
| With inner shell electrons | Characteristic radiation<br>- Energy = difference between the higher shell electrons that drop in to fill the hole in inner shell<br>- important is the L- and M-shell electrons filling in the K-shell spaces, giving characteristic Kα (59.3 keV) and Kβ (67.6 keV) radiation |
| With the nucleus | Major x-ray spectrum (~80%)<br>- Electron travelling close to the nucleus experiences acceleration and loses energy (‘braking’) |

## Line focus principle

- foreshortening of the focal spot length down the central ray

- Actual focal spot ($F$) = physical area on anode struck by electrons
    - Determined by length of filament and width of focusing cup
- Effective focal spot = width of x-ray beam leaving anode
    - Determined by anode angle and actual focal spot as
    $$
    f = F \sin{\theta}
    $$

### Effects of anode angle on x-ray beam

| Smaller anode angle | Larger anode angle |
| --- | --- |
| 7-9° | 12-15° |
| Small focal spot | Larger focal spot |
| Higher resolution and lower unsharpness | Lower resolution and higher unsharpness |
| Lower anode loading (heating) | Higher anode loading |
| Higher anode heel effect | Lesser anode heel effect |
| Smaller field coverage | Larger field coverage |
| Used in CT and fluoroscopic C-arms | Used in general radiography with large detectors (17”) |

## Anode heel effect

- decrease in intensity of beam that has to pass through the anode
    - ↓ intensity of beam at anode-end compared to cathode-end of tube
- More prominent with:
    - Shorter anode angle
        - Greater part of beam passes through the anode
    - Shorter source-to-image distance 
        - For same detector width, there is greater divergence
    - Larger collimation
        - Larger difference in extremes of beam

### Clinical applications of anode heel effect

- Chest: diaphragm towards cathode, lung apex towards anode
- Mammography: pectoralis towards cathode, nipple towards anode
- KUB: pelvis towards cathode, abdomen towards anode
- Ankle: joint towards cathode, forefoot towards anode

### Factors affecting x-ray beam quality

| Factor | Principle | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tube current | ↑ number of electrons hitting anode → ↑ x-ray photons | Increase in number of photons<br>No change in mean, maximum or characteristic energy |
| Tube voltage | ↑ energy of photons hitting anode → ↑ x-ray photons and their energy | Increase in number of photons<br>Increase in mean and maximum photon energy<br>No change in characteristic energy |
| Anode material | ↑ atomic number → ↑ electron interactions with anode atoms | Increase in number of photons<br>Increase in mean and characteristic energy<br>No change in maximum energy |
| Filtration | ↑ filtration → ↓ low energy photons in spectrum | Decrease in number and maximum energy of photons<br>Increase in mean energy of photons<br>No change in characteristic energy |
