## Generations of CT imaging

### First generation CT

- “Rotate-translate”
- Pencil-thin x-ray beam
- Tube and detector translate together → rotate together by 1°
- 5 min per slice

### Second generation CT

- “Rotate-translate”
- Fan beam over a 30° detector array
- Tube and detector translate together → rotate together by 6°
- 30-90 s per slice
 
### Third generation

- “Rotate-rotate”
- Fan beam over a larger array (30-40°)
- Tube and detector rotate 360° (made possible by slip ring technology)
- 0.3 s per slice
- Most commonly used system today

### Fourth generation

- “Rotate-stationary”
- Fan beam over a 360° detector array
- Tube rotates 360° while detectors stay stationary
- Not popular as:
    - higher radiation dose
    - expensive due to need of hundreds of detectors
    - antiscatter collimation not possible

### Electron beam CT

- “Stationary-stationary”, electron beam CT
- Electron beam is swept around a target stationary anode ring, with a rapidly moving electromagnetic field
- Allowed for ultrashort cardiac and pulmonary artery imaging
