### RCC syndromes and associated pathology
:::trivia
- Syndromes are usually autosomal dominant.

| Syndrome | Associated RCC subtype |
| --- | --- |
| VHL | CLear cell RCC |
| Birt-Hogg-Dube | oncocytomas to chromophobe RCC |
| TSC (also sporadic) | AML, rarely RCC |
| Hereditary papillary RCC | Papillary RCC type 1 |
| Reed (leiomyoma) | Papillary RCC type 2 (aggressive) / collecting duct / FH-deficient |
| HNPCC | Transitional cell carcinoma |

- Subtypes associated with specific patient populations:

| *TFE3* rearranged<br> Xp11, *MiT* translocation | Paediatric RCCs |
| Medullary | Sickle trait, SMARC1 deficient |
:::

### Imaging modalities for RCC

- US – initial detection
- CT renal mass protocol with CT chest 
    - choice of Ix
    - omit chest in T1a
- MRI – problem solving tool:
    - venous tumour extension
    - small renal mass characterisation
    - syndrome surveillance?
- Biopsy – indicated when surgery is not planned upfront
    - active surveillance of small renal non-clear cell carcinomas
    - systemic therapy
    - ablation

### Staging of RCCs

| T staging | N and M staging |
| --- | --- |
| T1 – ≤7 cm [1a: ≤ 4 cm, 1b: 4-7 cm]<br>T2 – ≥7 cm [2a: 7-10 cm, 2b: > 10 cm]<br>T3 – renal vein, perirenal fat or pelvicalyceal system [T3a],<br> infradiaphragmatic IVC [T3b],<br> supradiaphragmatic IVC [T3c]<br>T4 – adrenal or beyond Gerota’s fascia | N1: any node <br>M1: any distant metastasis |
