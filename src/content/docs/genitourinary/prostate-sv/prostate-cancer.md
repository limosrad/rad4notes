## Indications for imaging and intervention in prostate cancer

:::trivia

**Indications for MRI**

- In patients with PSA 3-20 ng/mL
    - preferred over population-adjusted risk calculators, and additional serum markers
- Before prostate biopsy in patients with suspected organ-confined disease
- Not indicated before biopsy in:
    - clear evidence of locally advanced disease on DRE
    - those not for curative treatment
- MRI is not a screening tool

**Indications for biopsy**
- PIRADS 4/5 – targeted biopsy with perilesional sampling
- PIRADS 3 – determine clinical suspicion (family risk, PSA density < 0.10 ng/mL/cc):
    - ↓: omit biopsy, do PSA monitoring
    - ↑: targeted biopsy with perilesional sampling
- PIRADS ≤ 2 – omit biopsy, do PSA monitoring (PSA density < 0.20ng/mL/cc and no family history)

**Biopsy technique**
- Trans-perineal > transrectal
- Perineal / rectal cleansing is essential before biopsy
- Omit antibiotics in transperineal approach in patients without infection risk
- Antibiotics (fluoroquinolones ± others) essential in others

**Further imaging**
- if unfavourable intermediate risk / high risk 
    - Gleason 3+4 with PSA > 10ng/mL or Gleason 4+3 and above
        - PSMA PET/CT (choice)
        - CECT abdomen + bone scan (if PSMA PET/CT not available)

:::
