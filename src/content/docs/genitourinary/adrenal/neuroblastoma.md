## Introduction
- third most common childhood cancer 
- most common extracranial solid tumour of childhood
- most commonly diagnosed cancer in infancy
    - 41% within 3 months
    - Median age 19 months

## Pathology
- Origin from primitive sympathetic ganglion cells (neural crest cells)
- Site: adrenal (46%), extra adrenal suprarenal (18%), posterior mediastinum (14%), rarely in neck, pelvis and other locations
    - Metastasis to: lymph nodes, bone marrow and cortex, liver
- Serum markers: ↑ vanillylmandelic acid (VMA) and homovanillic acid (HVA) (75%)
- Mandatory tissue sampling with bilateral bone marrow aspirates and trephine biopsies
    - Except: &lt; 6 months of age with L1 adrenal tumours &le; 3.1 cm in diameter if solid or &le; 5 cm if at least 25% cystic component 
- HPE: Small round blue cell tumour with Homer-Wright rosettes
- IHC: neuron specific enolase, S100, chromogranin
- Molecular: 
    - N-MYC amplification in 20% - poor prognosis (alone usually elevates to high-risk stage)
    - Hyperdiploidy (DNA index > 1) – better prognosis in < 18 month
    - Other important: 11q aberration, ALK and PHOX2B mutations

## Clinical features
- Abdominal mass and distension: most common presentation
    - Others: renovascular hypertension due to compression, thoracic scoliosis and airway compression
- Proptosis and periorbital ecchymosis: retrobulbar metastasis
- “Raccoon eyes”: compression of ophthalmic / facial veins
- Bone pain: metastasis
- Cord / cauda equina compression: neural foraminal extension
- Pancytopenia: bone marrow involvement
- Opsoclonus-myoclonus syndrome:
    - Dancing eye movements, rhythmic jerking, ataxia
    - Autoimmune phenomenon
    - Detected early, however neurologic improvement may be poor even after complete resection of small tumours
    - IVIg can cause initial resolution, however relapse is common
    - Residual neuropsychiatric abnormalities even after tumour cure
- Watery diarrhoea: paraneoplastic VIP secretion, resolves after tumour cure

## Imaging protocol (NCCN 2024)
- CECT chest abdomen pelvis OR MRI (&plusmn; contrast)
    - Suspected spinal involvement: MRI 
    - Suspected brain / orbit involvement: CECT / MRI
- 123I-MIBG (preferably SPECT): in all patients
- FDG/PET if MIBG non-avid disease or suspected mixed-avidity disease, except if:
    - &lt; 6 months of age with L1 adrenal tumours &le; 3.1 cm in diameter if solid or &le; 5 cm if at least 25% cystic component
    - Optional: PET/CT or PET/MRI
    - DOTATATE, DOTANOC and DOTATOC PET/CT are gaining popularity as they have higher resolution compared to MIBG, and can be done in single day visit (MIBG is done over 24 hours)
- Optional: 99mTc-MDP (bone) scans if MIBG non-avid or tumour excised
    - Isolated uptake requires biopsy confirmation

## Imaging findings

### USG
- Solid heterogeneous mass with calcification in 30-90% masses
- Inferior displacement of kidney in an adrenal mass
- Encasement and displacement of vasculature rather than invasion
- Congenital neuroblastoma on antenatal imaging:
    - 90% from adrenal – differentials: adrenal haemorrhage, sequestration
    - Usually, favourable staging and spontaneous regression in 1st year
- USG screening in infancy does not reduce mortality
- Modality of choice for image-guided biopsy

### CT and MRI
- Heterogenously enhancing solid mass crossing midline and displacing vessels, with metastasis to nodes, liver, bone and skin
    - Vascular relations can be contact, encasement, or flattening
        - Contact: < 50% circumferential contact
        - Encasement: > 50% circumferential contact – IDRF 
        - Flattening: ↓ diameter with partially visible lumen
    - Solid organ relation is described as “infiltration”: if there is no well-defined fat layer between organ and tumour
    - Lymph nodes: regional or non-regional
- WB-MRI: role in detecting bone metastasis, sensitivity comparable to Tc-MDP scan, specificity lower as cannot distinguish disease activity

### MIBG and FDG-PET
- Semiquantitative scoring of skeletal metastasis:
    - SIOPEN score – Europe
    - Modified Curie score – US
    - Prognostic value in high-risk disease
    - Can be used as surrogate marker of treatment response
- 1 distant MIBG avid site – metastasis 

## Staging and management

### Image-defined risk factors 
- Important as presence of anyone upstages tumour to L2 and into intermediate- or high-risk treatment groups.

| Region / Category | Image-Defined Risk Factor (IDRF) |
| --- | --- |
| Ipsilateral tumour extension<br>within two body compartments | neck–chest, chest–abdomen, abdomen–pelvis |
| Neck | encasing carotid &plusmn; vertebral body &plusmn; internal jugular vein <br>extending to base of skull<br>compressing the trachea |
| Cervicothoracic junction | encasing brachial plexus roots<br>encasing subclavian vessels &plusmn; vertebral &plusmn; carotid artery<br>compressing the trachea |
| Thorax | encasing the aorta &plusmn; major branches<br>compressing the trachea &plusmn; principal bronchi<br>Lower mediastinal tumour, infiltrating the costo-vertebral junction between T9 and T12 |
| Thoracoabdominal | encasing the aorta &plusmn; SVC |
| Abdomen &plusmn; pelvis | infiltrating the porta hepatis &plusmn; the hepatoduodenal ligament<br>encasing branches of the SMA at the mesenteric root<br> encasing the origin of the celiac axis &plusmn; of the SMA<br> invading one or both renal pedicles<br>encasing the aorta &plusmn; IVC<br> encasing the iliac vessels<br>pelvic tumour crossing the sciatic notch |
| Intraspinal extension | intraspinal extension in any location + invasion of &gt; one-third of the axial spinal canal, &plusmn; the perimedullary leptomeningeal spaces not visible, &plusmn; abnormal spinal cord signal intensity |
| Adjacent organ infiltration | infiltration of adjacent organs and structures: pericardium, diaphragm, kidney, liver, duodeno-pancreatic block, and mesentery |
| Conditions to be recorded, but not considered IDRFs | multifocal primary tumours<br>pleural effusion, with or without malignant cells<br>	ascites, with or without malignant cells |

### INRGSS stages
| Stage | Description |
| --- | --- |
| L1 | Localized tumour not involving vital structures as defined by the list of IDRFs and confined to one body compartment |
| L2 | Local-regional tumour with presence of one or more IDRFs |
| M | Distant metastatic disease (except stage MS) |
| MS | Metastatic disease in children younger than 18 months with metastases confined to skin, liver, &plusmn; bone marrow |

### Treatment groups
- based on INRGSS stage, age, presence of MYCN mutation, other factors (e.g., ploidy, INPC (pathologic classification), histology, extent of resection, symptomatic).

| Risk Group | Definition (typical Features) | Treatment Summary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low risk |- INRGSS L1 without MYCN amplification<br>- INRGSS L1 with MYCN amplification but completely resected<br>- INRGSS MS but asymptomatic, < 12 months, with favourable histology and DNA index > 1 without segmental chromosomal aberration | Observation ± surgery (if safe and complete) → surveillance |
| Intermediate risk | Local-regional disease (typically INRG stage L2) with one or more IDRFs or<br>- Metastatic disease in infants with favorable biology (excluding high-risk features)<br>- No MYCN amplification | Chemotherapy ± surgery based on response → surveillance |
| High risk | Metastatic disease (INRG stage M) in older children or<br>- Any stage with MYCN amplification <br>- Unfavorable histology &plusmn; adverse genomic features | Induction: chemotherapy with locoregional surgery<br>Consolidation: High dose chemotherapy with autologous stem cell rescue → RT<br>Post-consolidation: anti-GD2 immunotherapy |

### Response assessment
- by RECIST criteria (very good response category removed) 
- single longest diameter rather than tumour volume (currently).

## Differential diagnosis

:::differentials
Neuroblastoma vs Wilms' tumour on imaging
| Feature | Wilms’ | Neuroblastoma |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Age | 2-3 years |	< 2 years |
| Renal effects | “Claw sign” |	Displacement |
| Calcification | < 10%	| 85% |
| Aorta | Displacement | Encasement, uplifting |
| Midline extension | ± | ++ |
:::

Other ganglionic origin tumours
- All are derived from neural crest cells and show similar features on imaging.
    - Neuroblastomas are the most malignant and usually arise in ages &lt; 5.
    - Ganglioneuromas are benign and usually arise in ages &gt; 10.
        - Calcification less common than neuroblastoma
        - Whorled appearance on MRI
    - Ganglioneuroblastomas are intermediate between the two.
