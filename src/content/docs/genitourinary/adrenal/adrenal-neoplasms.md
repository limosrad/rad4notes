## Phaeochromocytoma

### Introduction
- Pheochromocytoma = adrenal medullary paraganglioma 
    - arising from chromaffin cells in the adrenal medulla. 
- Closely related tumors in extra-adrenal sympathetic and parasympathetic paraganglia are classified as extraadrenal paragangliomas (EAPs)
- Typically secrete both norepinephrine and epinephrine, but norepinephrine is usually the predominant catecholamine	

### Aetiopathogenesis

- Usually are sporadic, but syndromic phaeochromocytomas in 25-30%:

- 10% malignant
    - Direct extension into (liver, kidney, pancreas)
    - Nodal (most common) or distant etastases (eg, bone, liver, or lung)

### Clinical presentation

- Asymptomatic (3-5% of incidentalomas) middle aged adults
- Related to catecholamine excess: hypertension, palpitations, headache
- Labs: elevated plasma or 24-hour urine fractionated metanephrine levels

### Imaging modalities

- USG:
    - Initial modality for an abdominal mass
- Adrenal protocol CT:
    - For initial characterisation of adrenal mass
- MRI:
- Nuclear medicine scans:
    - 131I-/123I-MIBG scintigraphy is commonly used for pheochromocytoma, but suffers from drawbacks
        - Limited spatial resolution,
        - difficulty in detection of small tumours (< 1.5–2.0 cm) or
        - large tumours with extensive necrosis/haemorrhage,
        - lack of tracer uptake in some tumours, and
        - interference with certain medications, leading to false-negative results
    - 68Ga-SSTR PET/CT (somatostatin receptors): superior to MIBG and newer 18F-fluorodopamine or 18F-DOPA scans
    - 18F-DOPA PET/CT (storage and uptake of catecholamines): newer

### USG
- Heterogeneous and well-encapsulated, with hypervascularization at colour Doppler
- Early arterial pattern of enhancement at CEUS

### CECT
- Round or oval masses of similar attenuation to the surrounding soft-tissue structures at unenhanced imaging, with a reported mean attenuation of 35.9 HU ± 9.8
- Scattered punctate calcifications can be seen in up to 10% of cases
- Range of sizes is 1-10 cm with mean of 5.5 cm
- High overlap with other lesions due to variable degrees of lesion degeneration, necrosis, calcification, fibrosis, cystic change, and intracellular lipid degeneration

- Adrenal protocol CT
    - Absolute washout > 60% and relative washout > 40% similar to adenoma in 17-67%
    - Differentiating from adenoma:
        - Lesion attenuation of greater than or equal to 40 HU at nonenhanced CT
        - lesion attenuation of greater than or equal to 160 HU at portal venous phase CT,
        - lesion attenuation of greater than or equal to 70 HU at 15-minute delayed CT,
        - intralesional cystic degeneration at portal venous phase CT and 15-minute delayed phase CT

### MRI

- T1: may be hyperintense
- T2: Classic homogeneously “light bulb”–bright: infrequently encountered and accounts for as few as 11% of cases. Majority of homogeneous lesions are less bright than cerebrospinal fluid and tend to match the spleen in signal intensity
- In- and opposed: no signal drop unless fatty degeneration
- DWI: for nodal and liver metastasis
- MRS: most prominent resonating peak at 6.8 ppm, with other resonance peaks at 2.7, 3.16, and 3.8 ppm, attributable to catecholamines and catecholamine-derived metabolites
- Proximity to diaphragm makes it technically challenging
- T1 C+: Homo- or heterogeneous enhancement depending on internal components of fibrosis, necrosis, hemorrhage, and cyst formation
- Most commonly observed pattern (39%): heterogeneous enhancing lesion with multiple high-signal-intensity pockets or cysts as evidenced by areas of T2 prolongation
- Pheochromocytoma is the differential diagnosis of low-attenuation adrenal lesions, lesions with avid washout, and lesions with a dominant cystic component, depending on the clinical scenario and laboratory findings

## MEN syndromes

A group of AD syndromes, each featuring a collection of endocrine tumours.

### MEN 1 (Wermer syndrome)

Major features:
1.	Parathyroid adenomas (95%)
2.	Pancreatic NET (40%–80%)
    - gastrinomas are more common; 
    - usually multiple causing Zollinger Ellison syndrome
3.	Pituitary adenoma (30%)
    - usually functioning (60% are prolactinoma) and locally aggressive.

### MEN 2A (Sipple syndrome)

1. Medullary thyroid carcinoma (>99%)
    - often aggressive and multifocal
    - Occurs in adolescents or young adults
2.	Phaeochromocytoma (50%)—bilateral in 50%
3.	Parathyroid hyperplasia (20%–30%)
4.	Other associations
    - Hirschsprung’s disease
    - cutaneous lichen amyloidosis

### MEN 2B (also known as MEN 3)
1.	Medullary thyroid carcinoma (>99%)
    - aggressive and multifocal
    - Occurs in young children
2.	Marfanoid appearance (>99%)
3.	Mucosal neuromas (>99%)
    - on the lips, tongue, conjunctiva, nose and larynx
4.	Phaeochromocytoma (50%)
5.	Intestinal ganglioneuromatosis (40%)
    - resulting in megacolon

### MEN 4
1.	Parathyroid adenoma
    - hyperparathyroidism in 80%
2.	Pituitary adenoma (37%)
    - less aggressive than in MEN 1
3.	Neuroendocrine tumours of lung, GI tract and pancreas 
4.	Other rare tumours: adrenal, thyroid
