## Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours

### Introduction and epidemiology

- Solid neoplasms, constitute ~2% of pancreatic tumours
    - Second most common solid pancreatic tumours after PDAC
- Sporadic or associated with syndromes in 10%: 
    - MEN1, VHL, NF1, TSC and glucagon cell adenomatosis

### Pathology

WHO 2017 classification of pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms (panNENs):
- Well differentiated pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour (panNET)
    - Less proliferative, less atypia, less necrosis, intense synaptophysin and chromogranin A positivity
- Poorly differentiated pancreatic neuroendocrine carcinoma (panNEC)
- Mixed neuroendocrine non-neuroendocrine neoplasms (miNEN)

| Type | Mitoses per 10 HPF | Ki-67 proliferation rate % |
| --- | --- | --- |
| panNET G1 | <2 | <=2 |
| panNET G2 | <20 | 3-20 |
| panNET G3 | >20 | >20 |
| panNEC | >20 | >20 |
| miNEN | Each tumour ≥ 30%, both components high grade |

### Clinical presentations
- Occur in middle aged adults with M = F
1. Nonfunctioning: usually asymptomatic, 
    - if symptomatic are large with mass effect at presentation
2. Functioning: depends on the type of hormonal excess (see [differentials](#differentials))

### Imaging

- For detection, characterisation, localisation, staging, invasion, metastasis.
- panNETs are staged via a separate TNM staging system (9th edition, 2024) compared to panNECs which follow the same system as PDAC.

### USG
- Well defined hypoechoic mass, anywhere in the pancreas, with increased vascularity
- Liver metastasis – hyperechoic > targetoid > hypoechoic
- Intraoperative USG – for
    - Localising non palpable tumours
    - Relationship with adjacent vessels and pancreatic duct
    - Deciding between enucleation vs distal pancreatectomy
- Endoscopic USG – investigation of choice for small tumours
    - Tissue sampling
    - Relationship with MPD
    - Surveillance in MEN1 and VHL
- Contrast enhanced endoscopic USG:
    - can accurately distinguish panNETs (uniformly enhancing) from PDACs and PNECs (heterogenous enhancement)

### CT

- Protocol:
    - Pancreatic protocol CT with IV and neutral oral contrast
        - Technical: 120 kVP, 200 mAs, 0.5 s rotation, 1.5 mm collimation with section thickness 3 mm, at a pitch of 0.75 with 1.5 mm increments
        - Oral: 500 mL 30 min before examination, 250 mL immediately before examination
        - Scan from D11 to iliac crest
    - Phases
        - Non-contrast (not needed in dual energy acquisition)
        - Early arterial (25 s post injection)
        - Pancreatic (40-45 s post injection)<br>These two can be combined in a single late arterial (30-35 s) phase in usual DPCT protocols
        - Portal venous (60-70 s post injection)

- Findings:
    - Homogenous arterial phase hyperenhancing lesions that remain hyperenhancing in portal and subsequent phases (if any)
    - Larger tumours can show central necrotic changes (18% functional and 54% non-functioning tumours) or calcific changes (22% cases)
    - Liver and nodal metastases are arterial phase hyperenhancing and hypoenhancing in portal venous phases. Ring enhancement may also sometimes be seen
    - DECT – low energy images can increase contrast between hyperenhancing tumour and pancreatic parenchyma

- Advantages of CT:
    - Best modality for resectability, vascular invasion and nodal / systemic spread

### MRI

- Protocol:
    - Routine coronal T2FS, axial T2 and T2FS, axial T1 in and opposed, DWI at b-0, 400, 800 with ADC, MRCP (2D radial FSE thick sections and 3D FSE parallel imaging with MIPs)
    - Multiphase contrast 3D GRE FS sequences
    - Axial pre- and post-contrast arterial (20-40s), portal venous (45-65s) and equilibrium (3-5 min) phases
    - Coronal post-contrast  2 min post injection

- Findings:
    - T1: hypointense, no intravoxel fat
    - T2: hyperintense, no macroscopic fat suppression. Liver mets are T2 intense.
    - DWI: highly sensitive for small lesions, nodal and liver metastasis
    - ADC < 1.21 × 10<sup>-3</sup> mm<sup>2</sup>/s: sensitive for grade 2 and 3 panNETs
        - Tumour to background ADC ratio > 1.03: predictive for grade 1 panNETs
    - MRCP: relationship with MPD to determine enucleation vs resection
    - Contrast:
        - Intense arterial phase enhancement
        - Isointense in portal venous and delayed phases

- Advantages of MRI:
    - Excellent for detection and characterisation of lesions
    - Excellent for nodal and liver metastasis (DWI)
    - Excellent for pancreatic ductal relationship (MRCP)
    - Excellent for predicting grade: > 2cm, ill-defined margins, heterogeneous enhancement, hypoenhancement, low ADC vales predict a high grade

- Nuclear medicine

    - Somatostatin receptors are target for radiopharmaceuticals
        - Positive scan &rarr; candidate for peptide receptor randionuclide therapy (PRRT)

    - <sup>111</sup>In-pentetreotide SPECT/CT imaging (OctreoScan):
        - radioactive labelled somatostatin analogue &rarr; somatostatin receptors (50%–80% of panNENs)
        - Sensitivity: 77%, &uarr; in well differentiated, &darr; in poorly differentiated

    - <sup>68</sup>Ga DOTATATE PET/CT with 68Ga-tetraazacyclododecane tetraacetic acid (DOTA):
        - &uarr; spatial resolution, sensitivity compared to 111In-pentetreotide imaging
        - Krenning score can be used to grade tracer uptake for PRRT.

    - 18F-FDG PET/CT

        - &uarr; sensitivity for high-grade and poorly differentiated tumours that lack adequate somatostatin receptors, 
            - &uarr; SUVs &rarr; &uarr; Ki-67 index
        - Dual imaging may help to predict disease progression and prognosis
            - Dual-tracer PET/CT can be helpful to assess the tumour metabolic phenotype and
            - help to differentiate grade 3 panNETs (↑ uptake with 68GaDOTATATE), from grade 3 panNECs, (↑ 18F-FDG PET/CT uptake).
