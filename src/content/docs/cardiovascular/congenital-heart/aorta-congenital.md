## Cyanotic congenital heart disease

### Causes of cyanosis
Occurs when deoxygenated blood enters systemic circulation (**right-to-left shunt**).
- Low pulmonary blood flow
    - Tetrology of Fallot: RVOT stenosis &rarr; &uarr; RV pressures &rarr; blood flow across VSD into LV
- Parallel circulation (e.g., transposition of the great arteries)
    - TGA: deoxygenated blood in RV &rarr; aorta
        - incompatible with life without an ASD, VSD or PDA
- Intracardiac mixing
    - Truncus: common outflow of RV and LV

### Causes of decreased pulmonary blood flow
Presence of right atrial or ventricular outlet obstruction will cause decrease in blood flow into pulmonary arteries.
- Tetrology of Fallot (RVOT obstruction)
- Tricuspid atresia (tricuspid valve obstruction)
- Pulmonary stenosis (severe)
- EBstein anomaly (tricuspid valve obstruction)

## Acyanotic congenital heart disease

- **Left-to-right shunt** &rarr; &uarr; right heart blood volume &rarr; &uarr; pulmonary blood flow
    - Pulmonary plethora: enlarged central and peripheral pulmonary arteries and veins in all zones
        - Distinct from pulmonary oedema, which has fluid in interstitial and alveolar spaces

:::differentials
Chamber enlargement in different acyanotic congenital heart diseases

| Disease | Chambers enlarged |
| --- | --- |
| ASD | RA, RV |
| VSD | LA, LV, RV |
| PDA | LA, LV, aortic arch |
