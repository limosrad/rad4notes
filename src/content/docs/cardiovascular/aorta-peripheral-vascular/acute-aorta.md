## Introduction
- Spectrum of conditions affecting aorta with high mortality rate, hence a surgical / endovascular intervention emergency.
- Svensson classification of acute aortic syndromes:
    1. aortic dissection (AD)
    2. intramural haematoma (IMH)
    3. limited intimal tear (LIT)
    4. penetrating atheromatous ulcer (PAU)
    5. traumatic aortic injury

## Aetiologies
- Types 1-4 occur due to underlying causes affecting aortic intima:
    - Hypertension
    - Atherosclerosis
    - Collagen dysfunction (Marfan, EDS, Loeyz Dietz syndrome)
    - Bicuspid aortic valve
- Type 5 occurs usually due to blunt trauma, at the aortic isthmus – zone of transition between the relatively mobile (ascending and arch) and immobile (DTA) aorta.

## Pathology
- Intimal tear → blood tracking into false lumen → propagation of tear → into aortic branches causing malperfusion
    - Static compression – thrombosis of large false lumen of branch
    - Dynamic compression – ball valve effect of flap occluding lumen
- Classic classifications

:::differentials
| Classification | Ascending + descending | Ascending only | Descending only |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| De Bakey | I | II | III |
| Stanford | A | A | B |
:::

- SVS/STS (2020) classification:
    - A<sub>X</sub> – entry tear in Ishimaru zone 0 (ascending aorta including innominate artery origin) till zone X
    - B<sub>XY</sub> – entry tear in Ishimaru zone X (non-zero) till Y
    - I<sub>Y</sub> – unidentified entry tear with distalmost involvement of zone Y
- Tear Entry Mal-perfusion classification:

| Tear | Entry | Malperfusion |
| --- | --- | --- | 
| A – involving ascending aorta<br>B – not involving ascending aorta<br>Non-A non-B – involving arch but not ascending aorta | E0 – unidentifiable<br>E1 – involving ascending aorta<br>E2 – involving arch<br>E3 – involving DTA | M0 – no malperfusion<br>M1 – coronary<br>M2 – supra-aortic<br>M3 – spinal, visceral, iliac |

## Clinical features
- Presence of high-risk features in patients with acute chest pain should prompt investigation
    - High-risk condition (e.g., Marfan syndrome, known aneurysm)
    - High-risk pain (chest pain, tearing type)
    - High-risk examination feature (hemodynamic instability)
    - Presence of any 2 – CTA dissection protocol
    - < 2: rule out other causes (e.g., STEMI)
- Classification based on duration:
    - Hyperacute < 24 hours
    - Acute 24 hours-2 weeks
    - Subacute 2 weeks-3 months
    - Chronic beyond 3 months

## Imaging modalities
	
### Chest radiograph:
- Often one of the first investigations in an emergency
	
### Transthoracic echocardiography
- Valuable in ascending aortic dissection and in extremely unstable patients	

### CT dissection protocol
- choice of investigation, rapid, gives complete information required for surgical / endovascular management
- Two phases:
    - Non-contrast phase
    - Arterial phase post-contrast
- Coverage:
    - 3 cm proximal to aortic arch cranially
    - Just distal to iliofemoral bifurcation distally
    
### MRI
- Role in follow-up after surgery, not in acute setting

## Imaging findings

### Radiograph:
- Mediastinal widening
- Disruption of distinct aortic knob contour
- “Calcium sign”: separation of intimal calcification from wall > 5 mm
- Double density within aorta
- Tracheal deviation to right
- Deviation of nasogastric tube to right

### Imaging findings on CT

DISSECTION is the mnemonic for rapid reporting in an emergency.
- D = dissection or other AAS?
    - Identifying a dissection flap confirms dissection
    - IMH: mural haematoma on non-contrast sequence
    - PAU: ulcerated plaque extending into media
    - LIT: pseudoaneurysm due to focal intimal anomaly
- I = initiating primary intimal tear?
    - Important for surgical and IR management
    - Most common – close to sinotubular junction, just distal to left subclavian artery
- S = size of aorta and false lumen?
    - Orthogonal planes
    
:::differentials
| Findings | False lumen | True lumen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Caliber | Larger| Smaller |
| Attenuation | &darr; | &uarr; |
| Beak sign | + | - |
| Cobweb sign | + | - |
| Mural thrombus | + | - |
| Outer wall calcification | - | + |
| Relation to arch | Outer curvature | Inner curvature |

- “Beak sign”: acute angle between dissection flap and outer wall of false lumen
- “Cobweb sign”: thin stands representing torn media
:::

- S = segments of aorta involved
- E = extent of dissection and termination
    - Side branch extension – malperfusion
    - Into iliofemoral vessels – difficult access for interventions
- C = complications?
    - Aortic rupture, cardiac tamponade, coronary artery occlusion, cervical branch occlusion, renal or bowel ischaemia, limb ischaemia
- T = thrombus in false lumen
    - Prognosis: complete thrombosis > no thrombosis > partial thrombosis
- I = inspect true and false lumen morphology and complexity
    - For static and dynamic compression
- O = other possibilities
    - Looking for antecedent causes (e.g., bicuspid aortic valves, vasculitis)
    - Looking for other AAS
- N = Notification to clinical team as type A dissection is a surgical emergency


## Differentials

### Thrombosed false lumen of an aneurysm
:::differentials
| Finding | Dissection (thrombosed false lumen) | Aneurysm (calcified mural thrombus) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NCCT attenuation | High (within the false lumen) | Not high (within the false lumen) |
| Morphology | Usually an extensive lesion spiralling as it passes along the aorta | A more focal lesion maintaining a constant relationship to aortic wall |
| Inner border | Irregular | Smooth |
| Intimal calcification | Seen within the aorta	| Seen at the periphery of the thrombus |
| Aortic lumen | Often normal |	Large |

:::

## Management of aortic dissection

| Type | General management |
| --- | --- |
| A | Emergency surgical repair |
| B | Complicated → emergency TEVAR if favourable anatomy, else open surgical repair<br>Uncomplicated with high-risk features → elective TEVAR if favourable anatomy, else medical management<br>Uncomplicated without high risk features → medical treatment (beta-blockers + anti-hypertensives) |

### Complicated and high-risk dissection

| Complicated dissection | High-risk dissection |
| --- | --- |
| Aortic rupture<br>Organ malperfusion<br>Extension of dissection or progressive aortic enlargement on serial imaging<br>Refractory hypertension<br>Refractory pain > 12 hours | Aortic diameter > 40 mm<br>False lumen diameter > 22 mm<br>Entry tear > 10 mm<br>Entry tear at lesser curvature (↑ risk of type A conversion)<br>↑ aortic diameter > 5 mm<br>Haemorrhagic effusion<br>Evidence of malperfusion<br>Need for readmission<br>Reappearance of pain / symptoms |
