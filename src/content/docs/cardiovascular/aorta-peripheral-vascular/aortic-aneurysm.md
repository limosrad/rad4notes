## Introduction
- In general, dilatation > 2 SD and aneurysm > 50% diameter
- Aortic root → dilated > 4 cm, aneurysm ≥ 4.5 cm
- Ascending thoracic aorta aneurysm > 5 cm
- Descending thoracic aorta aneurysm > 4 cm
- Abdominal aorta aneurysm (AAA) > 3 cm

### Aetiologies
- Hypertension (~80 %)
- Infective / inflammatory (e.g., mycotic aneurysms → usually saccular)
- Atherosclerosis (less common, usually a PAU → saccular aneurysm)
- Genetic causes: Marfan, vascular Ehlers Danlos syndrome, Loeys Dietz syndrome, Turner, ACTA2 related
- Bicuspid aortic valves

### Clinical features:
- Asymptomatic, detected incidentally
- Non-specific abdominal pain
- Rupture → high mortality

## Imaging modalities
- Transthoracic echocardiography
    - first line imaging modality for diagnosis of thoracic aortic disease.
- Transabdominal ultrasound
    - screening for AAA recommended in ≥ 65 year old males who have ever smoked or have family history of AAA
- Cardiac CT and MR
    - modality of choice for diagnosis and follow-up
    - Post intervention CT is done after 30 days and 1 year to look for endoleak.

## CT imaging findings
- Aortic diameters 
    - measured inner-to-inner 
        - wall thickening &rarr; measured outer-to-outer
    - Aortic root diameter → sinus to sinus

### Assessment for endovascular treatment
- TEVAR:
    - Proximal and distal aortic seal at least 2 cm
    - Need for debranching of arch vessels, especially left subclavian artery
    - Degree of coverage of posterior intercostal arteries (for spinal artery supply)
    - Access site evaluation for large calibre access
- EVAR:
    - Proximal neck unfavourable if
        - Proximal aortic seal < 1.5 cm (device dependent)
        - Aneurysmal tortuousity > 60 degrees
        - Dilated neck (> 28 mm in abdominal aorta) or reverse tapering morphology
        - Proximal neck thrombus or calcification > 180 degrees
    - Presence of lumbar or mesenteric branch vessels
        - Increased risk of type 2 endoleak with lumbar arteries and IMA
    - Common iliac artery caliber > 7 mm 
        - needed for device delivery
    - Bilateral internal iliac status
        - May be occluded when limbs need to be extended in short common iliac arteries.
        - Pelvic ischaemia may result from occlusion.
        - Hypogastric artery preservation may be needed to reduce pelvic ischaemia.
    - Extension of aneurysm into CIA and EIA
        - May need limb extensions

### Differentials

:::differentials
Aortic aneurysms may also be caused by infections and inflammation.

| Syphilitic | Mycotic | Atherosclerotic | Inflammatory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Saccular | Saccular | Fusiform | Fusiform |
| Ascending and arch | Ascending | Any | Any |
| thin, dystrophic wall calcification | interrupted calcification, para-aortic gas collection and adjacent reactive enlarged LN	| cresenteric mural thrombus; ectasia of the remainder of the aorta with heavy atherosclerosis | mural thickening and extensive surrounding fibrosis |
:::

## Management

- Abdominal aortic aneurysm:
    - 3-3.9 cm: imaging every 3 years
    - 4-4.9 cm (men) or 4-4.4 cm (women): annual imaging
    - ≥ 5 cm (men) or ≥ 4.5 cm (women): 6-monthly imaging
- Surgical or endovascular treatment is indicated in:
    - Aortic root or ascending aorta ≥ 5.5 cm (≥ 5 cm if Marfan, ≥ 5.5 cm if bicuspid aortic valve)
    - DTA ≥ 5.5 cm
    - Degenerative TAAA ≥ 6 cm
    - AAA ≥ 5.5 cm in men and ≥ 5.0 cm in women or if symptomatic
    - Aneurysm growth by 0.5 cm/year or 0.3 cm/year for 2 consecutive years

### Choice of treatment

- Surgical > endovascular in:
    - Aortic root, ascending aorta or aortic arch aneurysm
    - Descending thoracic aneurysm if there is thoracoabdominal extension or proximal landing zone in Ishimaru zone 1 or 2.
    - Poor vascular access
- Controversy for AAA
    - ESC recommends EVAR as first line (class IIa) 
    - AHA/ACC recommends shared decision making
- Endovascular stent-graft placement is preferable in ruptured DTAA and AAA whenever possible, while open surgical repair is preferred in TAAA.

### Endoleaks after TEVAR or EVAR

:::differentials
| Type | Description | Management |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Loss of proximal or distal seal | Reintervention |
| II | Refilling via aortic branches (IMA, lumbar arteries) | if expansion ≥ 10 mm or decreasing neck → reintervention ± embolisation |
| III | Graft defect or component malalignment | Reintervention |
| IV | Porous graft (rare in modern grafts) | |
| V | Endotension leak | if expansion ≥ 10 mm or decreasing neck → reintervention |
:::

### Other complications
Ischaemia:
- Due to exclusion of critical mesenteric or posterior intercostal arteries (supplying artery of Adamkiewicz)
- Due to embolization of atherosclerotic plaque fragments
