## Indications for imaging in ischaemic heart disease

### Definitions in ACS

- Angina: obstructive CAD leading to chest pain and other symptoms on exertion
- Acute obstruction (due to complete occlusion) → myocardial ischaemia → myocardial infarction
    - Types of ACS:

| Type | Severe chest discomfort | Troponin elevation | ST elevation |
| --- | --- | --- | ---|
| Unstable angina | + | - | - |
| NSTEMI | + | + | - |
| STEMI | + | + | + |

:::differentials
| Impacted myocardium | ST elevation in | Problem vessel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anterior | V2-V4 | LAD diagonal branch |
| Septal | V1, V2 | LAD septal branch |
| Lateral | I, aVL, V5, V6 | LCX |
| Inferior | II, III, aVF | RCA → posterior descending branch |
| Posterior | V1-V4 (↓) | LCX or RCA posterior descending branch |
:::

### Role of imaging in acute coronary syndromes

- Patients needing urgent revascularisation and no role for cross-sectional imaging
    - STEMI, NSTEMI with haemodynamic instability or high-risk scores
- Indications of CT coronary angiography in ACS:
    - In low- and intermediate-risk patients
    - High pretest probability for &ge;2 of ACS, pulmonary embolism and aortic dissection
        - as part of a TROCT
    - Post-revascularisation in ACS

### Role of imaging in stable angina

- Determination of flow-limiting stenosis
    - Preferred in patients with low pretest probability of obstructive coronary artery disease.
    - CCTA &plusmn; CT-FFR
- Functional assessment of ischaemic and infarcted myocardium
    - Preferred in patients with high pretest probability of obstructive coronary artery disease.
    - 1st line: stress cardiac MRI, PET MPI, CT perfusion
    - 2nd line: SPECT MPI, dobutamine stress echo
    - 3rd line: dobutamine cardiac MRI

## Findings on CCTA

### CAD-RADS™ 2.0 - 2022
- Standardised reporting for stable angina:

| Category | Stenosis | Interpretation | Further investigation | Management |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0% | Absence of CAD | None | Reassurance &plusmn; non-atherosclerotic causes |
| 1 | 1–24% | Minimal non-obstructive CAD | None - consider other causes |- P1 and P2: preventive<br>- P3 and P4: aggressive preventive |
| 2 | 25–49% | Mild non-obstructive CAD | None - Consider non-atherosclerotic causes of symptoms |- P1 and P2: preventive<br>- P3 and P4: aggressive preventive |
| 3 | 50–69% | Moderate stenosis | Consider functional assessment | aggressive preventive + GDMT<br>I1: consider DSA, especially if frequent symptoms persist after GDMT |
| 4 | 4A - 70–99% stenosis<br>4B - Left main &gt;50% or 3-vessel obstructive (&gt;70%) disease | Severe stenosis | A: Consider DSA or functional assessment<br>B: DSA is recommended | aggressive preventive + GDMT |
| 5 | 100% | Total coronary occlusion or sub-total occlusion | DSA, functional &plusmn; viability assessment | aggressive preventive + GDMT &plusmn; revascularisation |

- Modifiers:
    - P - plaque burden based on segments involved and calcium scoring
    - N – non-diagnostic
    - HRP – high-risk plaque (≥ 2 of positive remodelling, spotty calcification, low attenuation plaque, or napkin-ring sign)
    - S – presence of stents
    - G – coronary artery bypass graft
    - I – ischaemic (based on CT FFR or perfusion)
    - E - exceptions (coronary dissection, anomalous origin of the coronary arteries, coronary artery aneurysm or pseudoaneurysm, vasculitis, coronary artery ﬁstula, extrinsic coronary artery compression, arterio-venous malformation).

### CT-FFR

- Fractional flow reserve = coronary artery pressure distal to stenosis / aortic pressure
- Measured 2 cm distal to a stenotic lesion:
    - Normal: >0.8
    - Borderline: 0.76–0.8
    - Abnormal: ≤0.75
- CAD-RADS 4 + abnormal CT FFR &rarr; DSA and revascularization
    - Those with normal FFR &rarr; safely deferred from DSA

## Findings on myocardial perfusion imaging

- The ultimate aim is to detemine which myocardium will benefit from revascularisation procedures.
    - Well established infarct will not benefit, while chronically ischaemic, hibernating myocardium will.

:::differentials
| Imaging | Stunned myocardium | Hibernating myocardium | Infarcted myocardium |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pathology | Acute MI | Chronic ischaemia | Old MI |
| LGE | - | - | Subendocardial + |
| Wall motion abnormality | + | + | + |
| Perfusion deficit | Reversible | Irreversible | Irreversible |
| Viability | + | + | - |
| Management | GDMT | Revascularisation | GDMT |

:::

### Stress cardiac MRI

| Qualitative | Quantitative |
| --- | --- |
| Perfusion defect | Myocardial perfusion reserve |

- Perfusion defect can be qualified as mild, moderate, severe; subendocardial, transmural; reversible.
- Myocardial perfusion reserve: Flow in stress/rest
    - < 1.5 – abnormal, 1.5-2 – borderline, > 2 – abnormal

### PET myocardial perfusion imaging

- Qualitative
![Qualitiative myocardial perfusion imaging](/rad4notes/images/flowcharts/nuclear-perfusion-imaging.svg)

- Quantitative: myocardial fractional reserve = flow at stress/flow at rest
    - MFR >2: High NPV (>97%) for high-risk obstructive CAD
    - PET MPI: Better assessment of balanced ischemia as seen with multivessel disease (MFR <1.5)
    - PET MPI: Better assessment of microvascular CAD (MFR <2)
