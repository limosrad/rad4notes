## Introduction
- Primary cardiac masses are < 10% of all cardiac masses
- Majority (>90%) are metastasis from lung cancer, breast cancer, melanoma, and RCC.

:::trivia
- Most common primary benign adult cardiac tumour: papillary fibroelastoma
    - Second most common: myxoma
- Most common primary benign paediatric cardiac tumour: rhabdomyoma
    - Second most common: fibroma
- Most common primary malignant cardiac tumour: angiosarcoma
    - Seconf most common: cardiac undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma
- Most common primary to metastasise to heart: lung cancer
    - Most common site of metastasis: pericardium
:::

## Aetiolopathogenesis

- Sporadic > syndromic
:::trivia
- Cardiac rhabdomyoma – tuberous sclerosis
- Cardiac myxomas – Carney complex
- Cardiac fibromas – Gardner syndrome, Gorlin-Gotz syndrome
:::
- Classified according to the WHO 2021 classification of cardiac masses

## Clinical features
- Asymptomatic, detected on thoracic imaging
- Arrhythmias – physical disruption of conducting system
- Embolisation into systemic and pulmonary circulation – e.g., stroke due to papillary fibroelastomas
- Constitutional symptoms – fever, malaise, weight loss

## Imaging Findings

| Tumour | CT Findings | MRI Findings | PET Findings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Papillary fibroelastoma | Most are too small to characterize; usually round and homogeneous | T1=, T2&darr; (high fibrous content) or &uarr;; T1 FS: no change; T1 C+: variable enhancement | Mild FDG avidity |
| Cardiac myxoma | Usually = to &darr; to myocardium; calcifications (coarse or punctate) in 14%; heterogeneous enhancement | Typically heterogeneous; T1=, T2&uarr;; T1 FS: no change; First-pass perfusion: no enhancement; LGE: heterogeneous | Variable FDG avidity |
| Cardiac rhabdomyoma | Homogeneous, hypoattenuating intramyocardial lesion and intracavitary extension | Homogeneous with all sequences; T1=, T2&uarr; (mild); First-pass perfusion: hypointense; LGE: isointense | No reports |
| Cardiac fibroma | Intramyocardial, commonly in the interventricular septum or free ventricular wall;  well-defined borders; calcifications are common | T1&uarr;&darr;; T2&darr;; First-pass perfusion: hypointense; LGE: delayed hyperenhancement &plusmn; a hypointense core | Mild FDG avidity |
| Cardiac lipoma | Precontrast: homogeneous fat attenuation; Postcontrast: no enhancement | Typical fat signal intensity; T1W&uarr;, T2&uarr;; T1 FS: &darr;; First-pass perfusion: minimal, if any; LGE: absent | No FDG uptake |
| Lipomatous hypertrophy of the atrial septum | Homogeneous fat attenuation, dumbbell shape, sparing the fossa ovalis; Postcontrast: no enhancement | Signal intensity characteristics identical to those of cardiac lipomas | Moderate FDG uptake due to brown fat; Some cases show no uptake |
| Primary cardiac paragangliomas | Noncontrast: heterogeneously hypoattenuating; Postcontrast: heterogeneous, intense enhancement | T1= to myocardium or &darr; (&uarr; with hemorrhage), T&uarr;; First-pass: dynamic contrast filling (usually heterogeneous), with central nonenhancing areas (tumor necrosis) | High radiotracer avidity on DOTATATE, 18F-FDOPA and FDG PET/CT and MRI |
| Cardiac angiosarcoma | Noncalcified mass on postcontrast images; heterogeneous enhancement | T1&darr;  (with &uarr; areas = intratumoral hemorrhage), T2 heterogeneous; T1 FS: no change; First-pass perfusion: avid; characteristic “sun ray” pattern when predominate along prominent vascular channels; LGE: heterogeneous | High FDG avidity |
| Cardiac leiomyosarcoma | Hypoattenuating; may appear as a filling defect if invading the mitral valve &plusmn; pulmonary vein; May have dystrophic calcification | T1=, T2&uarr;; T1 FS: no change; Postcontrast: high enhancement | High avidity |
| Metastases to the heart | Can be multiple; epicardial more common | T1&darr; (exception: melanoma, which is T1&uarr;), T2&uarr;; T1 FS: no change; First-pass perfusion: variable; LGE: hyperenhancement | High FDG avidity |

## Differential diagnosis
- Thrombi and vegetations: Non-enhancing on CT and LGE MRI images, no iodine on DECT
    - A long inversion time (approximately 550 msec at 1.5 T and 850 msec at 3 T) for avascular thrombus

:::differentials
| | Myxoma | Thrombus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contrast enhancement | + | - |
| DECT: iodine uptake | + | - |
| Morphology | Sessile or pedunculated | Usually sessile |
| Atrial fibrillation | Uncommon | Likely to be present |
| Atrial location | Commonly attached to the intra-atrial septum | Usually occurs in an enlarged chamber and the atrial appendage is commonly involved |
:::

:::differentials
Based on most common location of cardiac masses
| Tumour | Most common site |
| --- | --- |
| Papillary fibroelastoma | Aortic side of the aortic valve, often multifocal |
| Myxoma | LA, attached to fossa ovalis; tumour 'plop' into mitral valve |
| Rhabdomyoma | Right and left ventricles |
| Fibroma | Intramyocardial (ventricular septum or free wall) |
| Angiosarcoma | anterolateral free wall of the RA near the atrioventricular groove, sparing the interatrial septum<br>diffusely infiltrative mass |
| Leiomyosarcoma | LA > RA |
:::

## Management
- Benign tumours → surgical excision if symptomatic; excellent prognosis with little or no recurrence in non-syndromic cases
- Rhabdomyomas in TS usually regress in childhood
- Paragangliomas require lifelong surveillance
- Malignant tumours → surgical resection if amenable, else palliative chemotherapy; extremely poor prognosis
