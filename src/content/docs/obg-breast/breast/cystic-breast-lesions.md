### Findings

**Simple cyst**
- circumscribe round or oval shape with thin imperceptible wall and posterior acoustic enhancement, no internal vascularity
- T2 hyperintense, T1 hypointense, with no or minimal peripheral rim enhancement

**Complicated cysts**
- circumscribed mass with internal homogenous echoes (mobile), fluid debris level, or echogenic reflector
- Variable T1 and T2 signal with no or minimal peripheral thin enhancement

**Clustered microcysts**
- Cluster of anechoic subcentimetric circumscribed masses, with thin (< 0.5 mm) septations and no discrete solid component, though fluid-debris levels may be present
- T2 hyperintense, T1 hypointense, with thin or imperceptible septation, no or minimal peripheral rim enhancement

**Mixed solid cystic mass**
- thick wall or thick internal septation (> 0.5 mm), intracystic or mural mass, both solid and cystic components
- MRI: enhancement in septation or solid component.
- May be benign (abscess, fat necrosis, seroma) however will need correlation with history

### Management
- Simple cyst →  BI-RADS 2
- Complicated cyst:
    - Multiple bilateral or with multiple simple cysts →  BI-RADS 2
    - Mobile internal echoes or fluid-debris levels &rarr;  BI-RADS 2
    - Isolated uniform non-mobile low-level echoes →  BI-RADS 3
    - Palpable, increased vascularity →  BI-RADS 4
- Clustered microcysts:
    - Superficial and well visualised →  BI-RADS 2
    - Deeper or smaller →  BI-RADS 3
- Mixed solid cystic lesions →  BI-RADS 4

## Papillomas of the breast

- Intraductal lesions with central fibrovascular stalk
- Sporadic, but juvenile papillomatosis is known

### Pathology
Five lesions according to WHO classification of breast tumours:
- Intraductal papilloma ± atypical ductal hyperplasia
- Papillary DCIS
- Encapsulated papillary carcinoma
- Solid papillary carcinoma
- Invasive papillary carcinoma
The lack of myroepithelial cells is characteristic of invasion in the latter types

| Central papilloma | Peripheral papilloma |
| --- | --- |
| More common as more symptomatic | Less symptomatic |
| Usually single | Usually multiple |
| In central duct |	In peripheral ducts |
| Larger sized |	Smaller sized |
| Lower risk of malignancy |	Higher risk of malignancy |

### Clinical features
- Pathologic nipple discharge (unilateral spontaneous clear or bloody discharge from a single duct)
    - Benign intraductal papilloma is most common cause (35-48%)
    - Duct ectasia is second most common (17-36%)
    - Carcinoma (mostly DCIS) (5-15%)
- Palpable breast lump

| Mammography 1st<br>ultrasound for suspicious lesions | Mammography or ultrasound | Ultrasound 1st<br>mammography for suspicious lesions | Mammography and ultrasound |


MRI: if no overt lesion found on USG and mammography, or if radiologic-pathologic discordance with obtained specimen.

### Ultrasound findings

- Solitary or multiple intraductal masses with or without ductal dilatation
    - Solid component: hypoechoic or with cystic component
    Pedicle invaginating into duct if seen → vascularity on Doppler (characteristic sign)
    - Duct: distended → appearance of a mixed solid cystic lesion
- Complications: torsion or haemorrhage → echogenic debris within duct

### Mammography
- Mass, asymmetry, focal asymmetry, calcifications (predictor for associated DCIS), solitary dilated duct
- USG correlation is nearly always required

### MRI
- Intraductal mass with T1 ↓ and T2 ↑ and enhancement with washout or plateau kinetics
- Duct may be dilated with content giving it a T2 ↑ signal (T1 ↑ if haemorrhagic debris)
- Segmental non-mass enhancement → s/o DCIS
- Ongoing studies: high ADC (e.g., > 0.86 × 10-3 mm2/s) may predict malignancy

### Management
-  BI-RADS 4 lesions: tissue diagnosis sought
    - 4A: solitary dilated duct, mixed solid cystic mass without other aggressive features (e.g., angular margins, irregular shape)
    - 4B/4C: microcalcifications, other aggressive features
- As lesions are heterogenous, biopsy may be target at most suspicious part of mass, e.g., with microcalcification or segmental non-mass enhancement
- Vacuum-assisted breast biopsy (VABB) uses larger sized needles (8G or 11G) with vacuum assistance, to obtain larger amount of tissue for sampling
    - Can be done under USG, stereotactic or MRI guidance
    - Small lesions (< 1.5 cm with 11G, < 3 cm with 8G) may be completely removed and, if benign on HPE, VABB alone may be therapeutic
    - ~ 15% cases have residual lesion after VABB
- If malignant at HPE → duct exploration and excision of mass
