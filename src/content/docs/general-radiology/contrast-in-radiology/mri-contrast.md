## Gadolinium based contrast agents:

### Mechanism
- Paramagnetic, decrease T1 relaxation time for tissues taking up contrast
- Cannot be used for STIR sequences, can be used for FLAIR sequences.

### Classification

| Extracellular |  Hepatobiliary | Blood pool |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Redistribution into the extracellular space after intravenous injection with predominantly renal excretion | Partial hepatobiliary excretion (gadobenate and gadoxetate) | Only gadofosveset, due to albumin binding remains confined to intravascular compartment


| | Linear | Macrocyclic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ionic | Gadopentetate<br>Gadobenate (MultiHance)<br>Gadoxetate (Eovist/Primovist)<br>Gadofosveset (blood pool agent) | Gadoterate (Dotarem) |
| Non-ionic | Gadodiamide<br>Gadoversetamide | Gadobutrol<br>Gadoteridol<br>Gadopiclenol |

- Gadobenate: 3-5% biliary excretion &rarr; hepatobiliary phase at 1-1.5 hours
- Gadoxetate: 50% biliary excretion &rarr; hepatobiliary phase at 20 minutes

### Doses
For gadobenate and gadoterate, dose is 0.1 mmol/kg, i.e. 0.2 mL/kg.

### Allergy risk
- higher in asthmatics, with macrocyclic agents
- Management same as iodinated contrast allergy. No cross reactivity with iodinated contrast.

### Pregnancy and lactation

- Pregnancy: Routine use of gadolinium contrast is NOT recommended due to risk of foetal harm
    - Former FDA category C
    - Screening of women of reproductive age for pregnancy is recommended
- Lactation: No significant contrast excretion into breast milk
    - No recommendation to routinely stop breast feeding after contrast administration

### Nephrogenic systemic fibrosis

| Agent | Risk | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I	| High proven risk of NSF | Gadopentetate, gadodiamide, gadoversetamide |
| II | Little risk of NSF | Macrocyclic agents, hepatobiliary agents |
| III | Few unfounded cases of NSF reported, data inadequate | None as of April 2024 |

### ACR rules for contrast media administration
| Risk factors for NSF | Situation | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| prior dialysis | On dialysis / AKI | No eGFR needed, use group II agents |
| renal transplant | Inpatient | eGFR within last 2 days |
| single kidney | Outpatient without risk factors | No eGFR needed |
| kidney surgery | Outpatient with risk: | eGFR needed |
| renal cancer | - eGFR &gt; 45 | eGFR within last 2 days |
| diabetes mellitus (optional) | - eGFR &ge; 45 | eGFR within last 6 weeks |

- Minimum time for repeat administration (not given in latest edition): 
    - eGFR >= 30: 4 hours
    - eGFR < 30: 7 days

### Gadolinium deposition disease
In view of the theoretical neurotoxicity (deposition in globus pallidus and dentate nucleus), the use of linear GBCAs is no longer approved in Europe apart from specific uses like hepatobiliary agents or intra-articular contrast. 
The USFDA continues to approve all agents for use, with a warning label introduced for linear agents.
Ideally in young patients or needing repeat CE-MRIs, it is preferable to use macrocyclic agents.

## Organ specific contrast agents

### Intravascular or blood pool agents

- High molecular weight prevents distribution from vascular compartment to interstitium.
- In: assessment of perfusion, tumour neovascularity and associated permeability changes, prolonged vascular imaging
- Examples:
    - Gd-DTPA labelled albumin
    - Gd-DTPA labelled dextran
    - Chromium labelled RBCs
    - SPIO (also a reticuloendothelial contrast)

### Hepatobiliary contrast agents

- Gadolinium based: gadoxetate, gadobenate
- Magnesium based: Mn-DPDP
    - Parkinson-like syndrome, withdrawn

### Reticuloendothelial contrast agents

- Gadolinium oxide – toxicity precluded it from clinical trials
- Magnetite (Fe3O4)
- SPIOs and USPIOs
    - Superparamagnetic iron oxide (SPIO): intravenous agents, cause T2/T2* shortening
        - Examples: fexumoxide, ferucarbotran
    - Ultrasmall SPIOs: imaging of lymphatics, uptake in normal nodes causes T2/T2* shortening, metastatic nodes appear bright
        - Example: ferumoxytol

- Liposomal carriers for Gd and Mn contrast

### Tissue specific contrast

- Monoclonal antibodies (coated with magnetite)
- Metalloporphyrins
    - Mn derived porphyrin – appears to have good safety profile
- Nitroxides
- Ferrioxamine
