## Mediastinal compartments and contents

- On radiographs: superior, anterior, middle, posterior compartments
- On CT and MRI: prevascular, visceral and paravertebral compartments

## Prevascular / anterior mediastinal masses - differentials

| Solid masses | Cystic masses |
| --- | --- |
| Thymoma | Thymic cyst |
| Lymphoma | Lymphatic malformation |
| Germ cell tumour | Cystic teratoma |
| Retrosternal goitre | Retrosternal colloid goitre |
| | Pericardial cyst |

### Solid prevascular / anterior mediastinal masses

:::differentials
4Ts of solid prevascular mediastinal masses:
| Differential | Demographic | Imaging morphology | Additional features |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thymic neoplasms | Middle aged (> 40 years) | Off midline lobulated mass<br>Calcifications are rare and indicate &uarr; risk type | Pleural drop metastasis in &uarr; risk<br>Myasthenia, hypogammaglobunemia or pure red cell aplasia |
| Germ cell tumours | Children and young adults | Large midline mass<br>Calcification and fat in teratomas<br>Heterogenous in NSGCTs | Elevated tumour markers<br>Lung metastasis in seminomas and NSGCTs |
| Lymphoma | Most common mediastinal mass<br>Adult patients with bimodal distribution in HL | Bulky homogenous rounded masses<br>Compression of solid organs with infiltration late<br>Patent vessels coursing through masses | B symptoms present<br>Other nodal enlargement may be seen |
| Retrosternal goitre | | High density on NCCT<br>Continuity with thyroid gland | Ectopic mediastinal thyroid gland (without continuity) are rare |
:::

## Visceral / middle mediastinal masses - differentials

| Solid masses | Cystic masses |
| --- | --- |
| Lymph nodal masses<br>- TB<br>- sarcoid | Foregut duplication cysts<br>- bronchogenic cyst<br>- oesophageal cyst |
| Lymphoma | Thoracic duct cyst |
| Metastatic nodes | Pancreatic pseudocyst |
| Castleman's disease | Necrotic tumour |

### Important differential diagnoses

:::differentials
Foregut duplication cysts: occasionally T1&uarr; 
| | Bronchogenic cyst | Enteric cyst |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Subcarinal | Paraesophageal |
| Shape | Spherical | Tubular |
| Wall | Thin | Thick (due to presence of GI layers) |
| Meckel scan | Negative | Positive if gastric mucosa |
| Symptoms | Asymptomatic | Peptic ulceration |
:::

:::differentials
Egg shell calcification in mediastinal nodes
- Sarcoidosis
- Silicosis
- Histoplasmosis
- Lymphoma (postirradiation) 
- Blastomycosis
- Amyloidosis
:::

:::differentials
High attenuation (NCCT) &plusmn; avidly enhancing (CECT) nodes:
- Castleman's disease
- Kaposi sarcoma
- hypervascular metastasis (e.g., RCC)
:::

### Paediatric mediastinal masses

:::trivia
- Lymphomas are the most common mediastinal mass.
    - In children &lt; 10 years, NHL &gt; HL.
- Lymphatic malformations are the second most common.
- Germ cell tumours are the third most common.
    - &lt; 8 years: teratomas, yolk sac tumours
    - &gt; 8 years: mature teratomas, seminomas
        - almost exclusively in males
        - Klinefelter syndrome is a risk factor
:::
