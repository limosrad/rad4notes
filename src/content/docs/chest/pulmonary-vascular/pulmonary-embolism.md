## Diagnosis of acute pulmonary embolism

### ACC/AHA 2026 guidelines

:::trivia
![Pulmonary embolism diagnosis - ACC AHA 2026](/images/flowcharts/pulmonary-embolism-acc-aha-2026.svg)

- YEARS algorithm sets cut-offs for D-dimer depending on clinical signs of DVT, haemoptysis and PE being the first differential diagnosis.
- A pregnancy-adapted YEARS criteria can be used to identify patients who do not need imaging.
    - Imaging may be avoided in symptomatic pregnant patients with a positive lower limb compression USG.
    - If imaging is needed,  low-radiation dose CTPA is reasonable over low-dose VQ scan (class 2a recommendation).
:::

- Triage of patients is done into:
    - A – subclinical (incidental and asymptomatic)
    - B – symptomatic with low clinical severity score (PESI ≤ 85, sPESI = 0)
    - C – symptomatic with ↑ clinical severity score (PESI > 85, sPESI ≥1)
        - C1 – normal RV and biomarkers
        - C2 – abnormal RV or biomarkers
        - C3 – abnormal RV and abnormal biomarkers
    - D – incipient cardiopulmonary failure
    - E – cardiopulmonary failure

### ESC 2019 guidelines

:::trivia
ESC 2019 guidelines for investigation of acute PE:
- Pretest probability → low / intermediate → D-dimer → negative → ruled out
- Low/intermediate pre-test probability + positive D-dimer OR High pre-test probability
    - → CTPA (if non-pregnant)
    - → DVT scan (if pregnant) → CXR (if DVT scan negative) → CTPA (if CXR normal)s

ESC 2019 guidelines for management of acute PE:
- High-risk (cardiac arrest, obstructive shock or persistent hypotension) → systemic thrombolysis or catheter-directed therapy or surgery
- Intermediate and low risk → anticoagulation ± admission

:::

## Findings in acute pulmonary embolism

### Chest radiograph

- Named signs:
    - Fleischner sign – enlarged central pulmonary artery
    - Palla sign – sausage shaped right descending pulmonary artery
    - Hampton hump – wedge shaped peripheral consolidation representing infarct
    - Westermark sign – regional oligaemia
- None of these signs are sensitive; CXR has a greater role in excluding other diagnosis.

### Compression USG

- Findings suggestive of thrombosis:
    - Absent compressibility
    - Echogenic content in lumen
    - Absent colour flow
    - Loss of phasicity on Doppler

### Transthoracic echocardiography

- Excellent for looking for signs of RV dysfunction 
    - RV/LV end-diastolic ratio > 0.9
    - RV-EDD > 30 mm
    - TAPSE < 1.6 cm
    - Pulmonary acceleration time < 90 ms
    - RV/atrial gradient > 30 mmHg
    - Tricuspid systolic velocity > 2.6 m/s
    - RV free wall hypokinesia sparing apex (McConnell sign)

### CTPA

- Round central filling defects on cross section, with tram-track on long axis
    - May retain cylindrical shape from iliac vessels, or may get disintegrated in heart
    - If eccentric, form acute angles with vessel wall 
- Emboli can be classified into:
    - Central – involving MPA, RPA or LPA
    - Peripheral – segmental or distal PA
- Mild vessel wall expansion may be present
- Complete occlusion → no PA opacification beyond thrombus
    - Incomplete occlusion → usually at bifurcations
- Signs of RV dysfunction:
    - ↑ RV:LV ratio > 1.0: measured at site of maximum RV and LV diameter in axial images
    - Interventricular septal deviation
    - IVC reflux (not very specific)
- Signs of lung infarction:
    - peripheral airspace opacities with ↓ enhancement compared to atelectatic lung
    - “reverse halo sign”: central GGOs with peripheral well-defined consolidation → due to dual blood supply to lung
- Thrombus burden scores:
    - Qanadli score, modified Miller scores
    - Class 3 recommendation by ACC/AHA/SIR 2026: no benefit

:::differentials
| Acute pulmonary embolus | Chronic pulmonary embolus |
| --- | --- |
| Central | Eccentric |
| Acute angles with vessel wall if eccentric | Obtuse angles with vessel wall |
| "Polo mint" appearance | Pulmonary webs |
| Vessel expansion | Peripheral vessel pruning |
| No collaterals | Enlarged bronchial artery collaterals |
:::

:::trivia
- RV-LV ratio is the most significant prognostic indicator that can be seen on CTPA.
:::

### VQ scan

- Interpretation is based on the modified PIOPED II criteria
- Terminology:
    - Segmental defects: large [>75%], moderate [25-75%], small [<25%] wedge shaped
    - Match: V=Q; mismatch: V>>Q; triple match: V=Q=CXR abnormality

| Probability of PE | Findings |
| --- | --- |
| High (≥80%) | ≥2 large mismatched or equivalent moderate/large; normal CXR<br>Q >> CXR abnormality |
| Intermediate (20–79%) | NOT high or low probability |
| Low (<20%) | Non-segmental defects: small effusion blunting the costophrenic angle, cardiomegaly, elevated diaphragm, ectatic aorta<br>	Q << CXR abnormality<br> Matched ventilation and perfusion defects with a normal CXR<br>Small subsegmental perfusion defects with a normal CXR |
| Normal scan | No perfusion defects |

## Interventions in acute pulmonary embolism

### General management

- ACC/AHA 2026 guidelines: depends on risk stratification
    - A and B – oral anticoagulation
    - C – low molecular weight heparin + observe for worsening
    - D – LMWH + systemic thrombolysis / IR management (class 2b)
    - E – LMWH/UFH + vasopressor ± ECMO support
        - Systemic thrombolysis / IR management (class 2a)

### Catheter directed therapies

- Although costlier, catheter directed interventions (especially mechanical thrombectomy) provide rapid ↓ RV pressures compared to systemic thrombolysis, without ↑ bleeding risk
- Two types of catheter-directed interventions are available:
    - Catheter directed thrombolysis (CDL):
        - Thrombolytic administered after crossing occluded pulmonary artery branch
        - Dose: alteplase 2-5 mg bolus followed by 1 mg/hr slow infusion for a total dose of ~20-25 mg (divided for both catheters)
        - Dose of drug used is 20-25% of systemic dose
    - Mechanical thrombectomy:
        - Manual or mechanical suction of a thrombus
        - Rapid, single session offloading of RV strain
        - Mechanical thrombectomy is being validated across several ongoing trials. 
    - Other devices: ultrasound with CDL, Bashir catheter, AngioVac catheter, are in various stages of development and validation.

| Indigo Penumbra catheters | Inari Flowtriever |
| --- | --- |
| Electronic suction pump | Mechanical suction via 50 mL syringe |
| 8, 12, 14, 16 Fr catheters | 16, 20, 24 Fr catheters |
| Easier to steer with less cardiac injury risk | Relatively difficult to steer into pulmonary circulation |
| Rapid offloading however complete clot removal is often not possible | Near complete clot removal may be possible |

### IVC filter placement

- IVC filter placement is indicated in those patients where anticoagulation cannot be given (class 1 recommendation).
- Retrievable IVC filters are preferred, as they can be immediately removed when patient becomes eligible for anticoagulation (class 1 recommendation)
    - ↓ long-term adverse risk with IVC filters
