## Important differentials on a chest radiograph

### Unilateral opaque hemithorax
:::differentials
Unilateral opaque hemithorax &rarr; look for tracheomediastinal shift

| Ipsilateral shift | No shift | Contralateral shift |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lung collapse &plusmn; fibrosis | Consolidation | Diaphragmatic hernia (esp. on right ) |
| Post pneumonectomy | Effusion + collapse | Large pleural effusion |
| Pulmonary agenesis | Mesothelioma | Large neoplasm<br>- Ewing sarcoma of chest wall<br>- Pleural solitary fibrous tumour |

- Mesothelioma limits lung and mediastinal movement &rarr; may cause no or even ipsilateral shift.
:::

### Unilateral increased lucency of hemithorax

:::differentials
The most common cause is rotation &rarr; look for:
- asymmetry in vascular markings
- trachomediastinal shift

| Location | Causes |
|--- | --- |
| Chest wall | Mastectomy<br>Poland syndrome |
| Pleura | Pneumothorax |
| Lung | Proximal airway obstruction<br>Compensatory overinflation (e.g., after pneumonectomy)<br>Swyer-James syndrome<br>Bullae if unilateral<br>Congenital lobar emphysema |
| Vascular | Pulmonary embolism |
:::

## Lines and tubes on a chest radiograph

| Tube / line | Normal position |
| --- | --- |
| Endotracheal tube tip | within the mid trachea about 5 cm cranial to the carina (1.5 cm for kids) ± 2 cm with neck flexion and extension |
| Tracheostomy tube (does not move with neck flexion) | Tip between ½ and ⅔ of the distance between the stoma and carina ▸ the cuff should fill but not distend the tracheal wall |
| Swan-Ganz catheter | Right or left pulmonary artery 5 cm distal to MPA bifurcation (should not extend beyond the proximal interlobar arteries) |
| Central venous pressure catheter tip | between the medial end of the 1st rib (at the junction of the brachiocephalic vein and SVC), or within the SVC itself |
| Peripherally inserted central catheter line | Should terminate within superior vena cava |
| Intra-aortic balloon pump | tip should be located just distal to the left subclavian artery |
| Umbilical artery catheter | Caudal course → IIA → CIA → aorta. Tip either at (1) D6-D9 (no spinal artery) or (2) L3-L5 (below renal arteries) |
| Umbilical venous catheter | Cranial course → LPV → DV → IVC → IVC-RA junction |
| Mediastinal drains | Anterior mediastinum or posterior pericardium |
| Pleural tubes | In pleural space via mid axillary line (6th to 8th rib spaces). ▪ Anterosuperior (for a pneumothorax) ▪ Posteroinferior (for a pleural effusion) |
| Temporary pacing wires | Single chamber: (1) RA near the SA node (2) RV apex (3) RVOT<br>Dual chamber: (1) RA appendage (2) RV apex<br>Biventricular: as for a dual chamber pacemaker + (3) passing through the coronary sinus |
| V-A ECMO (cardiorespiratory support) | Venous outflow from SVC; arterial inflow into right CCA |
| V-V ECMO (respiratory support) | Two cannulas: separation 8-13 cm (< 8 → recirculation)<br>Double lumen: outflow should point into RA (towards tricuspid valve) |
