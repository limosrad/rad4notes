## Introduction

There are three guidelines for the management of solitary pulmonary nodules.

| Name | Used in |
| --- | --- |
| Lung-RADS | Screening in high-risk individuals |
| Fleischner society | Incidentally detected SPNs |
| BTS guidelines | In all patients > 18 years |

## Nature of a solitary pulmonary nodule

:::trivia
The following are more common in benign nodules:
- juxtafissural location
- oblong shape
:::

:::trivia
The following are strong predictors of malignancy:
- spiculation (strongest predictor)
- part solid nodules (higher risk than solid nodules)
    - the groung glass component may represent cellular spread or haemorrhage
- size > 3 cm or increasing
:::

:::


## BTS guidelines

:::trivia
Solid nodules:

- < 5 mm or < 80 cc → discharge
- ≥ 5 mm and < 6 mm → CT at 1 year
    - Stable on diameter → CT at 2 years
    - Stable on volumetry → discharge
    - VDT ≤ 400 → workup (biopsy)
    - VDT 400-600 → biopsy / surveillance
    - VDT > 600 → surveillance / discharge
- ≥ 6 mm or ≥ 80 cc → CT at 3 months
    - VDT ≤ 400 → workup
    - VDT > 400 → surveillance
- ≥ 8 mm or ≥ 300 cc → use Brock model for risk
    - < 10% → surveillance
    - ≥ 10% → PET/CT → Herder model
        - < 10% → surveillance
        - 10-70% → consider biopsy
        - ≥70% → staging ± biopsy and treatment
:::

:::trivia
Part solid nodules:

- < 5 mm or < 80 cc → discharge
- ≥ 5 mm or ≥ 80 cc → CT at 3 months (if no prior imaging or imaging follow-up < 4 years)
    - Resolved → discharge
    - Stable → use Brock model for risk
        - < 10% → surveillance (1, 2 and 4 years)
        - ≥ 10% → patient preference (biopsy ± treatment or surveillance)
    - Growth / altered morphology → biopsy ± treatment

:::

## Fleischner society guidelines (2017)

### Patient populations for use:
- patients > 35 years of age
- non-calcified nodules
- no history of malignancy

They are classified into low- and high-risk.
