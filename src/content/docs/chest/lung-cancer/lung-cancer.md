## Pathology

### Demographics

Lung carcinoma has 4 important pathologies. They are classified into small cell lung cancer (SCLC) and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).

:::trivia
| Type | Associations |
| --- | --- |
| Adenocarcinoma | Most common type (and ↑ incidence)<br>Most common type in non-smokers |
| Squamous cell carcinoma | 2nd most common (and ↓)<br>Strong association with smoking |
| Large cell carcinoma | |
| Small cell carcinoma | Strong association with smoking |
:::

## Imaging appearances of lung cancer subtypes
:::differentials

| Types | Location | Imaging appearance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adenocarcinoma | Peripheral | Small peripheral tumours<br>Pleural effusions<br>Can arise in scar tissue<br>Small lymph nodes |
| Squamous cell carcinoma | Central | Cavitation<br>Slowest growth rate<br>Most common tumour causing peripheral collapse / consolidation |
| Large cell carcinoma | Usually peripheral | Large peripheral masses<br>Early lymphadenopathy |
| Small cell carcinoma | Usually central | Fastest, most aggressive<br>Early, massive hilar nodes<br>Primary may be small in size<br>Peripheral collapse/consolidation |

:::

## Staging and management of lung cancer

The 9th edition of TNM staging is currently used. It is the most important staging asked in exams.

### T-staging of lung cancers

:::differentials
| T stage | Description |
| --- | --- |
| T1 | &le; 3 cm |
| T2 | &gt; 3 cm, &le; 5 cm<br>Visceral pleura and adjacent lobe invasion<br>Central invasion of hilar fat, main bronchus, or obstuction extending to the hilum |
| T3 | &gt; 5 cm, &le; 7 cm<br>**P**: parietal pleura, pericardium, phrenic nerves, chest wall, azygous vein<br>**Separate nodule in same lobe**|
| T4 | &gt; 7 cm<br>Mediastinum and its structures (thymus, great vessels, carina, trachea, oesophagus, vertebral bodies, spinal canal)<br>**Separate nodule in different lobe** |
:::

### N-staging of lung cancers

:::differentials

| N stage | Description |
| --- | --- |
| N1 | Ipsilateral hilar, peribronchial and intrapulmonary nodes | 
| N2 | Ipsilateral mediastinal nodes<br>N2a if single, N2b if multiple |
| N3 | Ipsilateral supraclavicular or scalene nodes<br>Contralateral mediastinal or hilar nodes |
:::

### M-staging of lung cancers
Pleural effusions and distal metastasis is M1 and upgrades prosgnostic stage to stage IV.

### Management

:::trivia
- SCLC: chemo &plusmn; radiotherapy + prophylactic CNS radiotherapy
- NSCLC: surgery wherever feasible &plusmn; neoadjuvant before for downstaging
    - Usually unresectable if > 3 cm mediastinal contact, > 90 degree aortic contact, T4 and N3 disease
:::

### Prognostic stages and stagewise management
Varies depending on setting.
- Stage I (&lt; T2a N0): wedge resection if feasible
- Up to stage IIIA (T4N1, T3N2a, T2N2a, T1N2b): lobectomy &plusmn; chemo
- &ge; stage IIIB (T1N3/T2N2b/T2N3/T3N2b/T4N2): chemo &plusmn; radiotherapy


## MCQs

:::mcq{answer="4"}

**A 52-year-old smoker presents with right sided ptosis and miosis. Chest radiograph reveals opacity in right apical zone. Subsequent workup including a biopsy demonstrates a 4 cm squamous cell carcinoma of right upper lobe with PET/CT showing avid uptake in ipsilateral right lower paratracheal nodes, and MRI showing brachial plexus involvement. What will be the T stage of this disease?**

1. T1
2. T2
3. T3
4. T4
5. Workup inadequate for T staging

**Explanation:** Brachial plexus involvement is T4 disease.

:::

:::mcq{answer="4"}

**Biopsy done for a supraclavicular node in a 55-year-old smoker turns out to be small cell lung cancer. What is the probable finding on chest imaging?**

1. 6 cm peripheral mass with small mediasinal nodes
2. 4 cm cavitating mass occluding the RUL bronchus and causing distal collapse
3. 2.5 cm peripheral mass with enlarged mediastinal nodes
4. 2 cm mass with enlarged bilateral mediastinal and hilar nodes.
5. Multiple 1-3 cm masses in bilateral lungs.

**Explanation:** Small cell lung cancer (also called as oat cell carcinoma) is notorious for extensive nodal and metastatic spread at presentation. Options 1, 2 and 3 describe adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma and large cell carcinoma respectively. Option 5 describes metastasis from distant primary.

:::
