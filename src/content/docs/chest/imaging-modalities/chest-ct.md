## Imaging protocols in chest CT

Many different protocols are used for different purposes.
| Protocol | Indications |
| --- | --- |
| NCCT | Interstitial and airway pathology |
| CECT | Parenchymal (neoplastic) and mediastinal pathology |
| LDCT | Lung cancer screening |
| ULDCT | Lung cancer screening |
| CTPA | Pulmonary vasculature pathology |
| CTA | Systemic vasculature (including bronchial vessel) pathology |

### HRCT or NCCT?

- Three requirements are fundamental for high resolution CT:
    - 1-2 mm (reconstructed) slice thickness
    - reconstruction with high frequency ('sharpening') filter
    - minimum motion artefact &rarr; single breath hold cpature
- Once upon a time, scanners were slow &rarr; to obtain fast images, thin sections were taken with axial scanning and interslice gap
    - Now, scanners are fast enough to acquire single breath-hold images of the thorax with helical (volumetric) scanning
- Thus, HRCT now refers to the image reconstruction done to volumentric raw data with sharp filters, with no interslice gap.

## CTPA

### Technique

### Caudocranial scanning
- To decrease streak artefact from rapidly inflowing contrast in the SVC
- To acquire lower thoracic images first, where respiratory motion is more visible

### Image viewing
- Thin reconstruction is done with 0.75-1.5 mm slices (depending on scanner capability).
- Technical adequacy checked with ROI in MPA
    - The mean HU should be above 250 HU.
    - If inadequate, DECT offers improvement in vessel contrast at the cost of SNR.
    - DECT reconstruction if available can be used for assessing lung perfusion.
- Optimal viewing window is a wide angiopgraphy window (e.g., W = 600, C = 100).

### Pitfalls
- Transient interruption of contrast
    - Deep intake of breath during scan &rarr; intrathoracic pressure &darr; &rarr; venous return &uarr; unenhanced blood from IVC &rarr; RA &rarr; MPA mixes with contrast enhanced blood in MPA
    - Gives a false impression of non-enhancing filling defect in the MPA
    - Remedy: pre scan patient instruction for shallow breathing
- Mimics of subsegmental embolism:
    - intrapulmonary lymph nodes, bronchi with mucus impaction
    - unopacified pulmonary veins
- Contrast dilution
    - Patent foramen ovale or other septal defects and L&rarr;R shunts
    - Can be repeated with mild inspiration for PFO, but usually not repeated for others
- V-A ECMO
    - May cause changes in the filling of the pulmonary arteries
    - Flow to be kept minimum during the scan

## CT angiography of chest

- Also termed as CT bronchial angiography
- Most common indication: evaluation of haemoptysis
- Simultaneous pulmonary and systemic arterial opacification may be obtained via a split bolus technique
