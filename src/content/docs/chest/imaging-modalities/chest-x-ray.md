## 1. Introduction

Chest radiography is one of the most commonly performed imaging examinations. It is used for the assessment of pulmonary, pleural, mediastinal and cardiac abnormalities.

## 2. Imaging Technique

### Standard projections

The two commonly encountered projections are:

| Projection | Description | Important consideration |
|---|---|---|
| PA | Posteroanterior | Standard erect chest radiograph |
| AP | Anteroposterior | Commonly used for portable examinations |
| Lateral | Lateral projection | Provides additional anatomical information |

### Technical considerations

A systematic assessment of image quality should include:

- Patient positioning and rotation.
- Degree of inspiration.
- Penetration or exposure.
- Inclusion of relevant anatomical structures.

## 3. Systematic Interpretation

A systematic approach helps minimize overlooked findings.

### Suggested sequence

1. Assess image quality.
2. Examine the airway and mediastinum.
3. Evaluate the lungs and pulmonary vasculature.
4. Examine the pleura and pleural spaces.
5. Assess the cardiac silhouette.
6. Examine the diaphragm and costophrenic angles.
7. Review the bones and soft tissues.

## 5. Testable Points

:::tip[TESTABLE POINTS]

- PA and AP projections differ in the relative magnification of the cardiac silhouette.
- Image rotation can affect the apparent mediastinal width.
- The costophrenic angles should be assessed systematically.
- Image quality should be evaluated before interpreting abnormalities.

:::


## Testable Trivia

:::trivia
- PA projection generally produces less cardiac magnification than AP projection.
- Patient rotation can alter the apparent mediastinal width.
:::

## Differentials

:::differentials
- Pulmonary edema
- Pulmonary infection
- Diffuse alveolar hemorrhage

Consider the clinical context and distribution of opacities.
:::


## MCQ

:::mcq{answer="2"}

**Which projection generally produces greater cardiac magnification?**

1. PA projection
2. AP projection
3. Lateral projection
4. None of the above

**Explanation:** AP projection generally produces greater cardiac magnification because the heart is farther from the image receptor.

:::
