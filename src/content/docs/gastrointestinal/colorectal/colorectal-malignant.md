## GI polyposis syndromes

| Syndrome | Abdomen | Extra-abdominal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Familial adenomatous polyposis** (FAP)<br>AD<br>APC gene | Colorectal adenomatous polyps (100%), colorectal cancer (100%), gastric hamartomatous polyps (90%), duodenal adenomas (80%–90%), desmoid tumor (10%–20%), hepatoblastoma | Congenital hypertrophy of retinal pigment epithelium, nodular thyroid gland, osteomas (20%–30%), papillary thyroid carcinoma (1%–3%), medulloblastoma, dental abnormalities (20%–30%), lipomas, sebaceous and epidermal cysts, nasopharyngeal angiofibromas |
| **Turcot’s syndrome**<br>AR | Colonic carcinoma, polyps | Medulloblastoma |
| **Gardner syndrome** | Polyposis in the GI tract | osteomas, fibromas, epidermoid and sebaceous cysts, desmoid tumours, congenital hypertrophy of retinal epithelium, supernumerary teeth, and papillary thyroid cancer |
| **Peutz-Jeghers syndrome** (PJS)<br>AD<br>STK11 | Hamartomatous small bowel polyps (60%–90%), colorectum (50%–60%), and stomach (15%–30%); colon (40%), stomach (30%), pancreas (11%–36%), small bowel (13%), biliary, gastroesophageal cancers, ovarian sex cord tumour with annular tubules (SCTAT); adenoma malignum of cervix; testicular Sertoli cell tumor | Mucocutaneous pigmentation (95%), breast cancer (32%–54%), lung cancer |
| **Juvenile polyposis syndrome** (JPS)<br>AD<br>SMAD4 | Hamartomatous colonic polyps (98%), gastric polyps (50%–90%), small bowel polyps (30%–50%), colon cancer, gastric cancer | Arteriovenous malformations (25%) in liver, gastrointestinal tract, lungs, and brain |
| **HNPCC (Lynch II)**<br>AD<br>MLH-1, MSH-2 | Colorectal (46%–61%), endometrial (34%–54%), ovarian (4%–20%), stomach(5%–7%), urinary tract, small bowel, pancreas, brain, prostate (4%–13%),and biliary tract cancers | Breast cancer, brain tumors (commonly, glioblastoma multiforme and astrocytomas), skin cancer, leukemia, myeloma, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, sarcoma |
| **Cowden’s syndrome**<br>AD<br>PTEN | Hamartomatous intestinal polyposis, ganglioneuromasDiffuse glycogenic acanthosis (>80%)Bilateral testicular lipomatous hamartomaRCCs (papillary and chromophobe RCC), endometrial cancer, early onset uterine fibroids | Lesions of the skin, mucous membranes, breast and thyroid<br>Bannayan Riley Ruvalcaba – lipomatosis<br>Proteus-like syndrome – diffuse soft tissue hypertrophy, bone hyperostosis |
| **Muir–Torre syndrome**<br>AD | Lynch syndrome variant (GI polyps and cancers in various sites) | Benign cutaneous sebaceous adenomas and keratoacanthomas |
| **Cronkhite–Canada syndrome**<br>acquired | Diffuse intestinal polyposis (usually colonic although the stomach and small bowel can be affected) | Associated with alopecia, skin hyperpigmentation and nail atrophy secondary to malabsorption; rapidly fatal |
