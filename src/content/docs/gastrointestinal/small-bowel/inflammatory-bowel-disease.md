## Pathology of IBD

| UC | CD |
| --- | --- |
| Relapsing and remitting episodes of inflammation limited to mucosal layer of colon |transmural inflammation, involvement from oral cavity to anus|
|Invariably involves rectum and extends proximally to involve contiguous areas in colon |Extensive involvement of right colon and small bowel is more common |

- Montreal classification of Crohn’s disease:
    1. Age of onset (A1 &le;16, A2 17-40, A3 >40)
    2. Location (L1: terminal ileum, L2: colon, L3: ileocolon, L4: upper GIT)
    3. Behaviour
        - B1: non-stricturing, non-penetrating;
        - B2: stricturing,
        - B3: penetrating
            - Sinus tract
            - Ulcer
            - Fistula
            - Inflammatory mass
            - Abscess
            - Free perforation
    4. Perianal disease (P: added to B1-B3)

## Imaging in IBD

### Modalities for IBD imaging

| Evaluate only the lumen | Evaluate lumen and bowel wall, and extraintestinal abdomen |
| --- | --- |
| Barium meal followthrough | USG |
| Barium enema | CT/MR enterography |

| Modality | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barium meal followthough<br>Barium enema | Dynamic evaluation of the whole gastrointestinal tract<br>Allows manual mobilization of the bowel | Ionizing radiationLimited in the evaluation of the intestinal wall<br>It does not allow panoramic view of the abdominal cavity. |
| USG | Low cost<br>Can be repeated as many times as necessary<br>Absence of ionizing radiation or contrast medium | Operator dependent<br>Limited because of intestinal gas distribution<br>Does not allow panoramic view of the abdominal cavity. |
| CT enterography | Thin section<br>Widely available<br>The optimal method for depicting extraluminal bowel gas and complex abdominal fistulas | Ionizing radiation<br>Lower contrast resolution of the bowel wall<br>Intravenous iodinated contrast material necessary |
| MR enterography | High contrast resolution<br>Allows functional evaluations<br>Can be performed without the use of intravenous contrast material<br>The optimal method to evaluate perianal fistulas | Higher cost and less availability<br>Contraindications related to the magnetic field and paramagnetic contrast material |

### Protocols in IBD

- CT enterography:
    - Oral neutral contrast – 300 mL of 20% mannitol in 1500 mL water
        - 450 mL 60 minutes prior
        - 450 mL 40 minutes prior
        - 225 mL 20 minutes prior
        - 225 mL 10 minutes prior
        - 250-300 mL just before scan
    - Optional ant peristaltic agent IV hyoscine 40 mg.
    - IV contrast – scan 45-50 s (enteric phase) or 60-70 s (portal venous phase) post injection (no difference in lesion detection)
- MR enterography: oral contrast same as CT, essential sequences:
    - CINE SSFP for bowel motility
    - Administer spasmolytic after this sequence
    - Axial T2 fat supressed and non-fat supressed
    - Coverage is diaphragm to perineum for basic perianal disease
    - Coronal T2 fat supressed
    - DWI: _b_\-0, 400, 800 with ADC
    - Dynamic contrast-enhanced T1-weighted FS 3D GRE
        - ECA 0.1 mmol/kg
        - acquire one coronal at 45 sec with two subsequent coronal acquisitions and one axial acquisition

### Imaging findings in bowel

- Definitions:
    - Stricture: bowel segment with luminal narrowing and unequivocal dilation (i.e., ≥3 cm) of the upstream bowel segment. 
        - Luminal narrowing: luminal diameter reduction of &ge; 50% compared with that of a normal adjacent bowel loop
    - Segmental mural hyperenhancement: &uarr; mural attenuation at CT enterography or &uarr; mural signal intensity at MR enterography on post-contrast images
        - Bowel should be noncontracted small bowel segment 
        - Compared with adjacent normal small bowel segments
    - Stratified mural hyperenhancement: hyperenhancement of the inner (bilaminar) or both the inner and outer (trilaminar) aspects of the bowel wall

| Site | Findings |
| --- | --- |
| **I**\-nflamed mesentery | Fat abnormalities (fat stranding, fibrofatty proliferation, also called creeping fat or fat wrapping)<br>Engorged vasa recta ('comb sign')<br>Lymphadenopathy(<1.5 cm) |
| **B**\-owel abnormalities | Thickening (mild 3-5 mm, moderate 5-9 mm, marked > 9 mm, suspicious for neoplasm > 15mm)<br>Segmental mural hyperenhancement (stratified, homogenous) <br>Permanent structural changes: loss of folds, stricturing |
| **D**\-isease complications | Luminal: malignancy, strictures, toxic megacolon<br>Extraluminal: fistula, abscess, perforation |

### Signs of disease activity in Crohn's disease
- MRI based disease activity scoring is in use (e.g., MaRIA, CDMI, MEGS)
- Bowel wall thickening with mural oedema (= stratified mural hyperenhancement) or ulceration is suggestive of active disease
    - T2 FS hyperintensity of bowel wall, T1 C+ trilaminar enhancement
        - T2 without FS: submucosal fat proliferation in chronic disease is also hyperintense
    - Wall thickening alone, especially if homogenous, may be due to fibrosis and stricturing.
    - On CT: stratified mural thickening with mucosal hyperenhancement may be due to oedema or fibrosis (both lower wall attenuation/enhancement) &rarr; cannot definitively give activity

:::trivia
Findings that indicate disease activity:
| Findings | More likely in active disease | More likely in inactive disease |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mural T2 signal | &uarr; (oedema) | &darr; (fibrosis) |
| Mural thickening | Stratified (trilaminar) | Homogenous |
| Mural T1 C+ | Early avid enhancement | Moderate or delayed enhancement |
| Mesenteric oedema | + | - |
| Lymph nodes &ge; 1 cm | + | - |
| Engorged vasa recta | + | - |
| Abscess and fistulae | + | - |
:::

## Differential diagnosis

### Crohn's disease vs ulcerative colitis
:::differentials
| Radiologic feature | Ulcerative colitis | Crohn’s disease |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small bowel involvement | Reflux ileitis only | +++ |
| Rectal involvement | Always | 50% |
| Multiple anal fistula | − | + |
| Aphthoid ulceration | − | +++ |
| Fissuring ulceration | − | ++ |
| Granularity | +++ | + |
| Transverse symmetry | Symmetric | Asymmetric |
| Longitudinal extent | In continuity | Discontinuous |
| Free perforation | + | − |
| Toxic megacolon | + | −/+ |
| Cancer risk | + | −/+ |
| Entero-enteric fistula | − | + |
| Submucosal inflammation | ++ in chronic disease | − |
| Mesenteric inflammation | −/+ | ++ |
| Enlarged lymph nodes | − | + |
| Fibrofatty proliferation | Meso-rectal only | ++ |
| Pathological process | Limited to mucosa | Transmural inflammation |
:::

## Management of active IBD

### General principles

1.  Site dependent
    - Ileal, ileocolic, colonic, other
    - Ileitis can cause malabsorption of vitamins or minerals.  
        - Two common nutritional deficiencies:
            - Lack of vitamin B₁₂ (causes peripheral neuropathy)
            - Lack of folate (causes anemia)

2. Disease behaviour

| Active  | Inactive |
| --- | --- |
| Aminosalicylates &plusmn; steroids | Dilation |
| Anti–TNF drugs | Strictureplasty |
| Immunomodulators | Resection |

3.  Extraintestinal manifestations: often requires more aggressive treatment

4.  Disease Activity and Therapeutic Response
    * Course of disease
    * Adverse effects
    * Response to drugs

### Intervention for abdominal abscesses

| Conservative Treatment | Percutaneous Intervention | Surgical Intervention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| - Small free-flowing peritoneal fluid or loculated collections <br>without wall in stable patients | - Favorable accessibility<br>- Single abscess<br>- Unilocular<br>- No communication with bowel<br>- No associated malignancy<br>- Thin contents | - Limited accessibility for percutaneous treatment (interloop location)<br>- Multiple abscesses<br>- Multilocular<br>- Communication with bowel<br>- Associated malignancy<br>- Thick debris |
