## Introduction

The following is a list of pathologies which have classical appearances on a barium oesophagogram.

| Pathology | Examples | 
| --- | --- |
| Rings and webs | Oesophageal webs, lower oesophageal rings |
| Diverticula | Pulsion, traction, intramural pseudodiverticulosis |
| GERD | reﬂux esophagitis, peptic strictures, Barrett oesophagus |
| Infections | Candidiasis, Herpes, CMV, HIV |
| Other oesophagitis | Drug-induced, radiation, corrosive injury |
| Motility disorders | Achalasia, diffuse oesophageal spasm, presbyoesophagus |
| Benign neoplasms | leiomyoma, mucosal papillomas, fibrovascular polyps |
| Malignancy | Squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma |
| Varices | Uphill, downhill |

## Rings and webs

![Oesophageal webs and diverticula](/images/diagrams/oesophageal-webs-diverticula.svg)

:::trivia
- Oesophageal webs are seen upper oesophagus.
    - Plummer Vinson syndrome = dysphagia + oesophageal webs + iron deficiency anaemia
- B rings (= mucosal rings) are seen in lower oesophagus.
    - Schatzki's rings = symptomatic B rings with oesophagus caliber < 13 mm
:::

:::trivia
Intramural pseudodiverticulosis
- dilated excretory ducts of deep mucous glands
- ﬂask-shaped outpouchings in rows parallel to the longitudinal axis of the esophagus
    -  may appear to float outside oesophageal wall, without apparent luminal communication
- clusters of pseudodiverticulae are often adjacent to a peptic stricture
:::

## GERD

Presence of reflux &rarr; look for sequelae

### Reflux oesophagitis
-  ﬁnely nodular or granular appearance with poorly deﬁned radiolucencies
    - fade peripherally due to mucosal oedema and inﬂammation
    - extends as a continuous area from the GE junction
- distal oesophageal ulcers
- longitudinal fold thickening
- inflammatory pseudopolyps

### Scarring and peptic strictures
- Scarring: ﬂattening, puckering, or sacculation with radiating folds
    - Oesophageal shortening: "step ladder" appearance of fixed transverse folds
- Peptic stricture: circumferential concentric smooth tapered narrowing
    - in the distal esophagus, almost always above a hiatal hernia

:::differentials
| Step ladder scarring | Feline oesophagus |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed transverse folds | Transient thin striation |
| Due to GERD | Due to longitudinal muscle contraction |
:::

### Barrett oesophagus
- Metaplasia: squamous epithelium &rarr; columnar epithelium
- Diagnosis made on UGIE and biopsy
    - Historic role of imaging &rarr; for triaging patients who need UGIE
- High-risk: 
    - reticular mucosal pattern resembling areae gastricae of stomach
    - distal aspect of a mid-oesophageal stricture
- Intermediate-risk: distal oesophagitis or strictures
- Low-risk: normal oesophagus

## Infectious oesophagitis

:::differentials
| | Candida plaques | Glycogenic acanthosis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finding | Linear irregular plaques | Rounded discrete plaques |
| Association | Immunocompromise | Age-related |
| Presentation | Odynophagia | Asymptomatic |
:::

![Infectious oesophagitis](/images/diagrams/oesophagitis-infectious.svg)

:::trivia
- CMV and HIV ulcers cannot be differentiated on double contrast swallow
    - Needs a UGIE &plusmn; biopsy as treatments are different
        - CMV: antivirals
        - HIV: steroids

:::

## Other oesophagitides

| Agent | Findings |
| --- | --- |
| Drug-induced | Depends on drugs (tetracyclines, NSAIDs, bisphosphonates)<br>Ranges from superficial erosions to deep ulcers and strictures |
| Radiation | Acute: granular appearance due to oedema; ulceration<br>Chronic: smooth concentric strictures |
| Corrosive | Acute: marked edema, spasm, and ulceration<br>Chronic: long-segment strictures |

## Motility disorders

### Achalasia

- Findings:
    - loss of primary peristalsis and incomplete relaxation of the lower esophageal sphincter
    - tapered beaklike narrowing of the distal esophagus (at GE junction)
    - "Sigmoid oesophagus" &rarr; massively dilated oesophagus

|       | Primary achalasia | Secondary achalasia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cause | Degenration of myenteric plexus | Tumour at GE junction |
| Narrowed segment | Short                | Longer                |
| Segment appearance | Smooth tapering | asymmetric, nodular, or ulcerated |
| Presentation | Long standing dysphagia | Recent onset dysphagia &plusmn; weight loss |

### Other motility disorders

| Pathology | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Diffuse oesophageal spasm | “corkscrew” esophagus, non-peristaltic contractions |
| Presbyoesophagus | asymptomatic non-peristaltic contractions in elderly |

- Most common oesophageal mass: carcinoma
- Most common benign oesophageal mass: leiomyoma
- Second most common benign oesophageal mass: [duplication cyst](/chest/mediastinum/mediastinal-masses#important-differential-diagnoses)
