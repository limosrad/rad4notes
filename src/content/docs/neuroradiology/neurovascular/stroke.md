## Imaging stategy in stroke

![Imaging strategy in stroke](/rad4notes/images/flowcharts/acute-stroke-imaging.svg)

## NCCT in stroke

- Rule out intracranial haemorrhage
- Rules out large core infarct > 1/3rd of MCA territory

### Early CT findings of stroke

- Obscured lentiform nucleus
    - d/t cytotoxic oedema, may be seen within 2 hours of onset
    - due to end-vessel supply
    - Overall NCCT has low sensitivity for infarct size in hyperacute and acute stage
- Insular ribbon sign
    - Hypoattenuating insular cortex (as it is a watershed area)
- Hyperdense MCA sign
    - Within 90 minutes of stroke onset
    - 100% specific, 30% sensitive
- Loss of grey white differentiation
    - 2-3 hours of symptom onset
    - d/t vasogenic oedema, indicates complete BBB disruption
    - Does not represent size of infarct

### ASPECTS score (Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score)

- One point is subtracted from initial 10 points for each region affected

    | Deep parenchyma | Cortex |
    | --- | --- |
    | Caudate head | Three ganglionic cortical regions |
    | Lentiform nucleus | Three supraganglionic cortical regions |
    | Insular cortex |  |
    | Internal capsule |  |

- Score < 7: lower chance of independent recovery after stroke

## CT perfusion in stroke

- General principle
    - $\text{CBV} = \text{CBF} \times \text{MTT} $
- Vessel stenosis or occlusion causes decrease in cerebral blood flow (CBF)
- Initially, cerebral autoregulation will try to preserve cerebral blood volume (CBV) by capillary bed vasodilation
    - This will increase transit time of blood in capillary bed
    - This tissue, with near normal CBV and &uarr; Tmax or MTT is salvageable with revascularisation
- Prolonged ischaemia will cause failure of autoregulation, lowering CBV and CBV and increase in infarct core size

### Interpretation of CT perfusion

| Pathology | TTP | CBF | CBV |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| None | = | = | = |
| Stenosis or occlusion (well compensated) | ↑ | = | = |
| At risk (‘penumbra’) | **↑ (> 6 s)** | ↓↓ (>30%) | ↓ (> 60%) |
| Irreversible / infarction | ↑↑ | **↓↓↓ (<30%)** | ↓↓ (<40%) |

## MRI in stroke

- DWI 
    - most reliable estimate of the ischemic core
    - correlates with irreversible injury
- DWI/FLAIR mismatch
    - DWI &uarr; with normal FLAIR suggests hyperacute infarct
    - May indicate extended window for IV thrombolysis
- DWI/perfusion mismatch
    - Large DWI area with relatively small infarct core on PWI
    - Indicates penumbra (salvageable ischaemic brain)

### Types of cerebral oedema

|  | Vasogenic oedema | Cytotoxic oedema |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cause | Tumour, abscess, haemorrhage, trauma | Ischaemia (e.g. stroke) |
| Mechanism | Disruption of the blood–brain barrier ▸ increased capillary endothelial permeability leads to fluid extravasation | Failure of membrane ATP-dependent sodium pumps ▸ accumulation of intracellular sodium and water |
| Imaging | Characteristic finger-like pattern with cortical sparing | Involvement of both cortex and white matter |
| DWI | No restriction | Restriction |

### Dating ischaemic stroke

|  | T1WI | T2WI | DWI | ADC |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hyperacute (0–6 h) | = | = | ↑ | ↓ |
| Acute (6 h to 4 days) | ↓ (+ oedema mass effect) | ↑ (+ oedema mass effect) | ↑↑ | ↓↓ |
| Subacute (4–14 days) | ↓↓ | ↑↑ | ↓ (may be ↑ secondary to T2 shine through) | = (pseudo-normalization) |
| Chronic | A smaller area of ↓↓ intensity + encephalomalacia | ↑↑ | ↓ (may be ↑ secondary to T2 shine through) | ↑ |
