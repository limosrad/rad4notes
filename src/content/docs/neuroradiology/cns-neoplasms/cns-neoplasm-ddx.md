## Approach to CNS tumours

![Approach to CNS tumours](/images/flowcharts/cns-tumour-approach.svg)

### Tumours vs tumour mimics

:::differentials
Tumour vs infarct:

|  | Tumour | Infarct |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grey matter changes | Usually centred on cerebral white matter; spares overlying grey matter | Often simultaneously involves cerebral cortex and juxtacortical white matter |
| Shape | Spherical or ovoid | Wedge or box shaped (with its base towards the brain surface) |
| Distribution | Not confined to a vascular territory | Confined to a vascular territory |
| C+ | Rare | Gyriform enhancement + |
:::

### Intra- vs extra-axial tumours

:::differentials

|  | Extra-axial tumour | Intra-axial tumour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Buckling” and medial displacement of the grey–white matter interface | + | - |
| CSF cleft separating the base of the mass from adjacent brain | + | - |
| Broad base along a dural or calvarial surface | + | - |
| Associated bone changes | Meningioma: hyperostosis<br>Dermoid cyst, schwannoma: bone scalloping, foraminal widening | - |
| Grey–white matter junction | Intact | Lost |

- Examples of extra-axial tumours: meningiomas (most common), calvarial and dural metastasis, dural solitary fibrous tumour, schwannomas, neurofibromas, epidermoid and dermoid cysts, chroid plexus lesions
:::

Intraventricular tumours:

| Tumour | Typical site |
| --- | --- |
| Colloid cyst | Foramen of Monro/third ventricle |
| Meningioma | Trigone of lateral ventricle |
| Choroid | Fourth ventricle |
| Ependymoma | Lateral ventricle (more common in children) and fourth ventricle |
| Neurocytoma | Lateral ventricles (involving septum pellucidum) |
| Metastases | Lateral ventricles, ependyma and choroid plexus |

## Common differentials of paediatric and adult CNS tumours

### Paediatric infratentorial

- Juvenile pilocytic low-grade cerebellar astrocytoma: most common
- Medulloblastoma: 33%
- Brainstem glioma: 20%
- Ependymoma: 10%
- Others:
    - extra-axial (e.g. dermoid and epidermoid cysts, schwannoma, neurofibroma and meningioma)
    - skull base (e.g. Langerhans’ cell histiocytosis, Ewing’s sarcoma and glomus tumours)

### Paediatric supratentorial

- Pleomorphic xanthoastrocytoma
- Hemispheric astrocytoma
- Embryonal tumours:
    - supratentorial primitive neuroectodermal tumour (PNET)
    - Giant cell subependymal astrocytoma (tuberous sclerosis)
- Neuronal and mixed neuronal/glial tumours:
    - Ganglioglioma, gangliocytoma
    - Desmoplastic infantile ganglioglioma
    - Dysembryoplastic neuroepithelial tumour
- Germ cell tumours

### Adult supratentorial

- Metastases
- Astrocytomas: ranging from a circumscribed astrocytoma through to a glioblastoma
- Oligodendroglioma
- Lymphoma
- Giant cell subependymal astrocytoma (tuberous sclerosis)
- Neuronal and mixed neuronal/glial tumours: e.g., a central neurocytoma
- Gliomatosis cerebri

### Adult infratentorial
- Metastases: most common
- Haemangioblastoma: the most common primary adult infratentorial tumour
- Astrocytic tumour: usually supratentorial but can occur within the posterior fossa
- Medulloblastoma: although typically a paediatric tumour, 20% can occur in adults
- Ependymal tumour: e.g. a subependymoma
- Neuronal and mixed neuronal/glial tumours
