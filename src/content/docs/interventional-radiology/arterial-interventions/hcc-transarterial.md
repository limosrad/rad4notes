## TACE

### Contraindications to TACE

- Poor hepatic function
    - Total bilirubin > 2 mg/dL
    - Serum albumin < 3 g/dL
    - Child-Pugh class C
- Poor portal vein flow or portal vein toxicity
    - Thrombosis
    - TIPS in situ
    - Arterioportal shunting
-  cTACE has likely lower rate of bile duct injury but ↑ postembolization syndrome


## TARE = SIRT

- Is cTACE superior to radioembolization?
    - No proven survival benefit of one over other, although radioembolization has shown ↑ time to progression
    - Given evolution of radioembolization data showing significant benefit of personalized dosimetry and dose thresholds, data are likely to evolve

### Radiation dose targets

- Conventional TARE for palliative intent
    - nontumoral liver dose ≤ 40 Gy (resin) & ≤ 75 Gy (glass)
    - tumour dose &ge; 205 Gy (ideally, &ge; 250 Gy)
- Radiation segmentectomy for curative intent
    - target dose: 400 Gy (glass)
- Radiation lobectomy (alternative to portal vein embolisation)
    - target dose to non-tumoural liver: 70-80 Gy (resin) or 140-200 Gy (glass)

### Contraindications to TARE

- Poor liver function (same as TACE)
- Excessive hepatopulmonary shunting following mapping study (> 20%)
    - Risk for radiation pneumonitis
    - Goal for < 30 Gy estimated pulmonary dose per treatment (healthy lungs)
    - Goal for < 50 Gy estimated cumulative lifetime pulmonary dose

### Complications of TARE

- Radioembolization-induced liver disease (REILD)
    - Generally manifests as ascites & hyperbilirubinemia (≥ 3 mg/dL) without biliary obstruction within 8 weeks of radioembolization
    - Pathologically form of sinusoidal obstruction/venoocclusive disease
    - Rx: supportive (diuresis), defibrotide, TIPS if severe decompensation
