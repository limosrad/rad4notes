## Grading systems for lower extremity arterial disease

### Clinical classifications

- Rutherford's classification for acute limb ischaemia

| Class | Classification | Description |

### GLASS for CLTI

- Global Limb Anatomic Staging System (GLASS)
- Steps to calculate:
    - Look for significant femoropopliteal (FP) disease (i.e., > 50% stenosis)
        - Femoral artery stenosis and total occlusion
        - Popliteal artery stenosis and total occlusion
        - Significant calcifications
    - Look for infrapopliteal (IP) disease
        - Determine a target pathway (ATA, PTA or peroneal)
        - Determine the degree of stenosis, total occlusion, and calcifications along this path
- Final stage:
    1. Short to intermediate length FP disease &plusmn; short IP disease
        - no or minimal popliteal disease
    2. Intermediate to long-length FP disease &plusmn; popliteal stenosis &plusmn; short to intermediate length IP disease
    3. Extensive FP or IP occlusions, popliteal total occlusion
- Predicts immediate treatment failure and 1-year patency
    | GLASS stage | Immediate failure | 1-year patency |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | 1 | < 10% | > 70% |
    | 2 | 10-20% | 50-70% |
    | 3 | > 20% | < 50% |

## Procedure trivia

### Access approach

- CFA or proximal SFA disease: contralateral retrograde CFA access
- Mid-SFA or more distal disease: ipsilateral antegrade CFA access
- Retrograde pedal / anterior tibial access: when both these fail
- Upper limb access: device length insufficient

## Techniques

1.  Bolia technique
    - antegrade subintimal angioplasty alone
    - deliberate entry into and exit from the subintimal space using a looped wire
2. SAFARI 
    - subintimal arterial flossing with antegrade-retrograde intervention
3. Pedal-plantar loop technique
    - wire through the dorsalis pedis and plantar arch 
    - recanalises a tibial artery retrogradely via the foot circulation
